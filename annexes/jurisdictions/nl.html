Annex: Netherlands#

This annex is general information, not legal, tax or insurance advice. Check current rules with a qualified local adviser.

What this annex is for#

This annex holds what the organizers of the 2026 conference in Maastricht reported about Dutch tax and ticketing. It is a stub. It records an experience, not a rule set, and it states no tax rate or threshold. Jurisdiction-specific VAT guidance needs a tax professional, and nobody has written it for this handbook.

Applies when#

  • jurisdiction=NL The conference takes place in the Netherlands, or tickets are sold by an entity subject to Dutch VAT.

What organizers reported#

Cross-border VAT as a top-level challenge#

The 2026 team was a company-based organizer whose tax home was in Germany, running an event in the Netherlands. The organizers reported that VAT became a major organizing challenge in that setup, including Dutch BTW on ticket sales (L-5001, S098). The full case note is under finance#case-notes, and this annex does not repeat it.

Buyer support load#

Buyers asked how to get VAT refunded or corrected. The team had to publish refund instructions in the middle of the ticket campaign (L-5001, S098). Plan this support load before the campaign opens, with an owner and a published answer.

Decide the regime before setting prices#

The organizers advise resolving the applicable VAT regime before you set ticket prices, especially when organizer and event are in different countries (L-5000, S098). The decision framing is in finance.

Who sells the ticket#

The debrief reported that the selling entity changes the final price for participants who cannot recover VAT (L-5002, S098). It also reported two claims that nobody verified: a university organizer may charge no VAT, and Foundation-sold tickets carry no EU VAT. They stay unresolved candidates in the requirements register (S106). Do not rely on either claim without written confirmation from an adviser. See ticketing and registration.

Risks#

  • Ticket prices are published before the VAT regime is settled and must be corrected later. Likelihood high, impact medium. Settle and document the regime with an adviser before the campaign opens.

  • Buyers cannot recover VAT and the support inbox fills with refund questions. Likelihood high, impact low. Publish a refund explainer on the ticket page and name an owner.

  • A reported exemption is assumed and then does not apply. Likelihood medium, impact high. Treat every unverified claim in this handbook as a question for the adviser.

Thin content#

Dutch VAT on conference tickets

No verified rule is in the handbook. The experience above is the only evidence.

Refund instruction template

thin | kind: single-source | needs: A sanitized, reusable buyer-facing VAT refund explainer and the questions it answered | who-might-know: The 2026 ticketing and support roles; the Foundation treasurer role | area: jurisdiction-annexes

One conference published refund instructions, and its text is not archived here.

University and Foundation as selling entity

thin | kind: single-source | needs: Written confirmation of whether a university or the Foundation can sell tickets without VAT, and under which conditions | who-might-know: Foundation board; Dutch university finance offices; a tax adviser | area: jurisdiction-annexes

Both claims are reported by one debrief and unverified.
