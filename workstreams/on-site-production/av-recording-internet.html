AV, recording and internet#

Why it matters#

Plone attendees work on laptops and expect the network to carry more than web and mail. When it fails on the first morning, the program and the mood of the event suffer.

Recording is the second half of the topic. Sessions nobody can watch afterward are lost to people who could not travel and to those who missed a parallel track. Recording takes hardware, hands and a named owner.

AV and video were the largest cost group at one past conference (as of 2010, S016), a network failure costs trust, and missing footage cannot be recovered. For live access by remote viewers, see Hybrid and online.

Applies when#

  • all Every conference needs a network, projection and a decision about recording.

Decisions you must make#

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-2016, L-4070, L-4174) Will you record sessions and publish them fast? Register row R-016 (verified-current, as of 2026-11-02) records the call-for-proposals guidance, which strongly encourages recording as many sessions as possible with fast turnaround, and treats live streaming as optional. (decide-by: T-9m)

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-3084, L-5025) Who owns recording, and who is their deputy? Give recording its own workstream and task list, separate from the program. (decide-by: T-9m)

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-1115, L-4096, L-2076) Can the network carry every attendee connecting at once, and is that in writing? Ask for capacity, address pool size and open ports before signing. Register row R-022 (venue provides reliable wireless for all attendees at once) is unverifiable as of 2026-11-02, so treat it as advice. (decide-by: T-9m)

  • OPTION Do you hire a wireless provider or run the access points yourself? Two recorded views disagree (L-1116, L-1117), and one specialist returned for two conferences (L-2036, L-2077). (decide-by: T-6m)

  • OPTION Do you pay for a dedicated internet line and your own power backup? Two conferences did (L-2076, L-3110). (decide-by: T-6m)

  • OPTION Which rooms and sessions are filmed, trainings included? Seattle prioritized tutorials in two rooms (L-1124, L-1125), Bristol filmed four rooms (L-2046), attendees asked for everything (L-1126), and a 2024 survey summary recommends exploring trainings (L-4208). (decide-by: T-6m)

  • OPTION Where do you host video? One team reports that PeerTube worked well (L-5027). (decide-by: T-3m)

How-to#

Before you sign the venue#

  1. Ask the venue for a written network statement: capacity for all attendees at once, address pool size, upstream bandwidth, open ports and whether non-work use is permitted. Done: the statement is in the planning folder.

  2. Ask what "install date" means to the provider: the day work starts or the time the network is live. Done: a written live-by time, earlier than the first session of the first training day.

  3. Get a direct phone line to the upstream network administrators, and see the wifi-failure playbook for what to do with it. Done: the number is on the production contact sheet.

  4. Name the recording owner and a deputy. Done: both names appear on the role map.

Before setup day#

  1. Rehearse recording with the actual hardware through capture and playback, and document file destinations and the upload handoff (L-5049; S100). Done: one test file played back from the real destination, and a one-page handoff note.

  2. Check projector brightness and screen size for the biggest room, plus a spare. Done: a projector list with spares.

During the conference#

  1. Test the network with many real devices at once on setup day. Done: the pool and the uplink hold under load.

  2. Confirm every evening that the day's recordings reached their owner and the uploader. Done: a tick on the daily readiness list.

After the conference#

  1. Convert and publish the raw footage within the agreed days. Done: the playlist is public and linked from the program page.

Check that no ports are blocked upstream.

[general]

lesson | scope: general | source: S015, S049, S066 | ledger: L-2016, L-4070, L-4174 | confidence: reported | status: active | basis: independent-conferences

Prefer fast publication of recordings over longer, higher-quality post-production.

[general]

lesson | scope: general | source: S098 | ledger: L-5025 | confidence: observed | status: active | basis: structural | reason: Capturing, converting and uploading video is hands-on work per session, whatever the community value.

Give recording its own workstream and accountable owner.

Four pieces of advice rest on one conference each:

  • Pay an accountable provider rather than run the wireless network yourself (L-1116; S009), contradicted in the case notes.

  • Record every talk even without post-production (L-1126; S088), set against prioritizing in the case notes.

  • Provide a bright projector per room plus a spare, and screens of about 3 by 3 meters (L-1145, L-1146; S009, as of 2006).

  • Do not learn video codecs and formats yourself, advice that ages fast (L-1127; S009).

Risks#

Wi-Fi fails on the first morning

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: high | mitigation: Get capacity, pool size, open ports and a live-by time in writing and keep a direct line to the upstream administrators | owner-role: network lead | playbook: wifi-failure

Failures hit the first days in 2006 and 2008.

The person who runs the network is unavailable

risk | likelihood: low | impact: high | mitigation: Name a deputy with access to every credential and contact before setup day | owner-role: network lead | playbook: key-person-loss

One conference lost its Wi-Fi owner two weeks before the event.

Recording has no owner and publication stalls

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Assign a recording owner and rehearse capture, file handoff and upload before setup day | owner-role: recording owner

Raw footage still needs conversion after the event.
