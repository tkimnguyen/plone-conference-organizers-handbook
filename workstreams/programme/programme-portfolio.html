Programme portfolio#

Why it matters#

The program is the reason people travel. Venue, tickets, marketing and sponsors all serve a set of sessions that someone has to design as a whole before the first call goes out.

Past conferences covered related technologies, accessibility, user experience, documentation and community organizing, and served as meeting places for special-interest groups such as universities and NGOs (L-4086, S096). A team that treats the program as a list of talks misses most of that range.

Without an explicit portfolio, trainings, talks, business days and sprints compete for the same rooms, volunteers and attention. Each product then gets its own call, price and audience by accident. The cost shows up as thin rooms, double-booked helpers and sponsors who cannot tell what they are paying to reach.

The Foundation, speakers, sponsors, the venue and volunteers are all touched by these choices. They fix the scope that CFP and selection, Scheduling and Keynotes and invited talks then work inside.

Applies when#

  • all Every conference has a program, and its shape drives every other workstream.

Decisions you must make#

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-4088, L-4213) Which products fill the week? Name each product (trainings, conference days, sprints, and any business track) with its own audience, owner and call. Bids and recaps from different conferences show the same week-long shape of trainings, conference days and sprints. (decide-by: T-12m)

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-2051, L-4213) Which session formats go beyond regular talks? Plan regular talks, lightning talks and open space as a set, because they serve different attendees. Attendees at one conference judged that mix well selected, and another delivered it at scale. (decide-by: T-12m)

  • OPTION How many days of conference talks? A three-day talk schedule is a suggested design in past calls for proposals, but no Foundation page confirms it (register row R-019, unverifiable as of 2026-11-02). (decide-by: T-12m)

  • OPTION Will another event share the program? Co-locating adds audience and shares costs, but it gives away program space (L-3112). See business-and-side-tracks for the 2025 co-location. (decide-by: T-12m)

  • OPTION How are sessions grouped into tracks? Options seen are by topic (L-4139) or by skill level, which scheduling covers. (decide-by: T-9m)

  • OPTION Which languages do sessions run in? One conference ran most sessions in English and a minority in the host region's language (L-4173). (decide-by: T-9m)

  • OPTION Is there a community strategy or roadmap session? A strategic open space produced a community roadmap (L-4111). (decide-by: T-6m)

How-to#

  1. Write three to five attendee goals as plain sentences. Done: the program committee has signed off the goals page.

  2. List every product of the week and give each an audience, an owner and a call date. Done: a one-page product table. Chapters: trainings, business-and-side-tracks, sprints, social-events-and-party.

  3. Derive session types from the goals, so every goal has at least one session type that serves it. Done: a table of goal, session type and format.

  4. Decide how sessions group into tracks and sketch the number of parallel rooms. Done: a track list handed to Scheduling.

  5. Reserve fixed slots before opening any call: the Foundation annual meeting, keynotes and social events. Done: the slots appear in a draft grid.

  6. Write one call per product from cfp-template, each with its own deadline, following CFP and selection. Done: calls published.

  7. Compare accepted sessions with the goals once selection ends. Done: a gap list, filled with invited sessions from Keynotes and invited talks.

Principles and general lessons#

No general lesson has passed the promotion rule yet. Two pieces of advice rest on one conference each and are tracked here until independent evidence arrives:

  • One organizing team advises a strong focus on sector-specific users, because education and government attendees came in especially strong numbers (L-1058, S009).

  • The same team advises more facilitated workshop sessions on shared community problems, led by a strong facilitator, with a broader part of the community in planning (L-1059, S009).

Risks#

A core audience has no sessions

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Compare accepted sessions with the written attendee goals before the schedule freezes and fill gaps with invited sessions | owner-role: program lead

Selection by merit alone can leave a goal, such as beginner content, uncovered.

Products compete for the same rooms and volunteers

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Give every product an owner and reserve its rooms and helper hours in the draft grid before any call opens | owner-role: program lead

Trainings, talks and sprints draw on one venue and one volunteer pool.
