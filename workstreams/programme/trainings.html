Trainings#

Why it matters#

Pre-conference trainings are one of the strongest draws of a Plone conference, and the Foundation requirements register lists them as part of the conference design (as of 2026-11). They bring newer community members in, and they give experienced people a way to teach. They are also where an unwritten promise can cost the most.

Trainers have received lodging reimbursement and a free ticket, and some have asked what the work earns them. The Foundation has no rule on any of this, so the first-time organizer meets the question late, with a published training offer already in the world. The 2026 debrief named conference-funded trainer lodging near the venue as the largest financial risk of the conference (S098). This chapter helps a team settle the terms before announcing the offer, and the money side is covered in finance. What the community expects from trainers is covered in community-and-speaker-expectations.

Applies when#

  • all The Foundation register expects pre-conference trainings at every conference.

Decisions you must make#

  • MUST (req: R-017; as-of: 2026-11-02) Will the conference include pre-conference trainings, and for how many days? The register records one to two days in the call for proposals, and the day count comes from the call only. (decide-by: T-12m)

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-4115, L-4189, L-5021) What does a trainer receive, and is it agreed in writing before the training offer is announced? Compensation is unregulated, and costs arise regardless of session length. Agree fee, ticket and travel terms with each trainer before any announcement. (decide-by: T-10m)

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-4115, L-5020) Who pays trainer lodging, and is it on site or nearby? Conference-funded lodging close to the training venue was an implicit expectation. Fix the lodging commitment before confirming the training offer. (decide-by: T-10m)

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-4068, L-4127, L-4200) Are the training classes included in the ticket price? Teams at several conferences included them, and the Foundation encourages inexpensive or included classes. Check the effect on ticket price and on trainer terms before deciding. (decide-by: T-9m)

  • OPTION If classes are sold separately, who registers the students? One conference let each trainer register students, which removed financial risk and confused some registrants. Handling registration centrally reduces the confusion. (decide-by: T-9m)

  • OPTION Do you write a quality floor into each trainer agreement? The 2026 survey analysis proposed hands-on exercises, stated prerequisites and stated outcomes after one attendee reported learning nothing; it is one voice, and the fix costs little (L-6022). (decide-by: T-10m)

  • OPTION Do you recruit an experienced community member as training lead? The call for proposals has said since 2018 that teams may wish to. (decide-by: T-8m)

How-to#

Plan the offer#

  1. Record the day count, the ticket model, the trainer terms and the lodging commitment in the Tailoring Record. Done looks like one entry that the treasurer and the program lead have both read.

  2. Recruit a training lead if you chose to. The call for proposals from 2018 says a team may wish to recruit an experienced community member for this role (L-4081, S055). Done looks like a named lead.

  3. Open the call for trainers with a quality check on proposals. Done looks like a call with published criteria and a review step.

  4. Label each session honestly: a session called a training or tutorial is hands-on, with prerequisites announced in advance. Done looks like a prerequisites line in every class description.

Announce and register#

  1. Release a preliminary program without talks, and send a survey on training registration before early-bird and regular tickets. The Foundation planning schedule places this in May (L-4038, S004, as of 2026-11). Done looks like survey results that size the rooms.

  2. Publish class descriptions, dates and rooms, and tell trainees where each class meets. Done looks like an email to every trainee and room signs on the first morning.

Run and close#

  1. Name a stand-in for every class, from experienced community members. Done looks like a list of stand-ins held by the training lead.

  2. If a trainer withdraws, follow the speaker-dropout playbook. Done looks like the class run by the stand-in or released with an apology.

  3. Check each training room for power outlets and a large screen before the first class. Done looks like outlets for every seat and a screen that shows code to the back row.

  4. Survey trainees after the conference. Done looks like a rating per class that you hand to each trainer.

Principles and general lessons#

[general]

lesson | scope: general | source: S009, S032, S049 | ledger: L-1089, L-3068, L-4068 | confidence: observed | status: active | basis: independent-conferences

Pre-conference training by experienced trainers is a major draw and good marketing, especially for newer community members, and it works best when classes are inexpensive or included in the ticket. An open call keeps the community involved, but it still needs quality control.

[general]

lesson | scope: general | source: S046, S050, S054, S073 | ledger: L-4114, L-4127, L-4140, L-4200 | confidence: reported | status: active | basis: independent-conferences

Several organizing teams have chosen to include training in the ticket price. The records show the choice repeated; they do not show its effect on trainer income or on ticket revenue.

Three further Seattle statements come from a single conference and are recorded in Case notes, not promoted: dropping three-hour tutorials (L-1094), handling training registration centrally (L-1095) and hands-on labeling of tutorials (L-1096).

Risks#

Trainer lodging and fee commitments exceed the budget

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: high | mitigation: Agree trainer terms and lodging in writing before announcing the training offer, and cost them at the worst-case number of trainers | owner-role: treasurer

The 2026 debrief named conference-funded trainer lodging near the venue as the largest financial risk. Costs arise regardless of how long a session runs.

A trainer does not appear

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Name a stand-in from experienced community members for every class before the first training day | owner-role: training-lead | playbook: speaker-dropout

One conference lost a presenter and relied on community members to cover the session.

Trainees cannot find their class

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: low | mitigation: Email every trainee the room and the prerequisites, and post room signs before the first morning | owner-role: training-lead

An attendee at one conference received no instructions and found no room signs.

Thin content#

No Foundation guideline on trainer compensation

thin | kind: gap | needs: A Foundation guideline on trainer fees, lodging and tickets, or a recorded decision that none will exist | who-might-know: Foundation board; Conference Committee; past organizers | since: 2026-11-02

An organizer asked the Foundation for such guidelines in 2023 (S070), and the 2026 debrief still describes compensation as unregulated (S098). This chapter offers a decision frame, not a rule.

Effect of included training on revenue and trainer income

thin | kind: single-source | needs: Revenue and trainer-income figures from a conference that included training, set against one that sold it separately | who-might-know: Treasurers of past conferences; Foundation board | since: 2026-11-02

The pattern of including training is documented across conferences, but its financial effect is documented nowhere in the archive.
