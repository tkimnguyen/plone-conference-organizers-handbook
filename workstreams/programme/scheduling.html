Scheduling#

Why it matters#

The schedule is the product attendees actually consume. It decides who can see which session, whether they can change rooms, eat and talk to peers, and whether the sessions they care about collide.

It is also where fixed obligations meet the program. The Plone Foundation holds its annual member meeting at the conference, so the grid has to reserve that slot before anything else is placed. Getting its length or its remote access wrong is hard to repair once speakers and attendees have planned around a frozen grid.

Late changes cost trust with speakers who have booked travel and with attendees who chose days by the published agenda. Sessions chosen in CFP and selection and shaped in Programme portfolio arrive here as a list that has to fit rooms, time and people.

Applies when#

  • all Every conference with more than one session has a schedule to build.

Decisions you must make#

  • MUST (req: R-015; as-of: 2026-11-02) Does the published schedule include the Foundation annual member meeting? Reserve a slot for the annual member meeting, which the Foundation holds once a year at the conference. Place it before the grid is built around other sessions. (decide-by: T-9m)

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-5026, L-4113) How long is the annual meeting slot, and does it need remote access? Resolve duration and remote-access expectations through the Foundation confirmation process before freezing the schedule (L-5026, S106). A 2026 debrief reports that a 25-minute slot proved insufficient, and register row R-034 holds the question as unverified as of 2026-11-02. (decide-by: T-3m)

  • OPTION How many sessions run in parallel? One team advises three to five in place of two (L-1078), another ran four rooms over two days (L-2041). Let room count follow program size and venue. (decide-by: T-6m)

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-1082, L-2097, L-4205) How long are changeovers and breaks? Plan more than five minutes between sessions and 30-minute main breaks. A 2024 survey asked for punctual sessions and enough breaks. (decide-by: T-6m)

  • OPTION Where do lightning talks go, and how long are they? Rows from one conference suggest an hour per session at three to four minutes a talk (L-2099, L-2100). Sessions filled the room and grew from day to day (L-2096). (decide-by: T-6m)

  • OPTION Which tool builds and publishes the schedule? One organizer advises paying for a hosted service over building one (L-3035). (decide-by: T-6m)

  • OPTION Which sessions are recorded or streamed, and does that constrain rooms or slots? Room choice and slot length follow recording needs; see av-recording-internet and hybrid-online. (decide-by: T-3m)

  • OPTION When does the draft go to speakers and the full agenda to the public? Circulate the draft to speakers first (L-1079) and publish as early as accepted sessions allow. (decide-by: T-3m)

How-to#

Before the grid#

  1. Collect the fixed slots: Foundation annual meeting, keynotes, meals, social events and the closing lightning talks. Done: a fixed-slot list with durations.

  2. Confirm the annual meeting's duration and remote access with the Foundation. Done: a written answer, dated, in the planning folder. The Foundation meetings page states that member meetings are held once a year and coincide with the conference (L-4063; S007, as of 2026-11).

  3. Check the candidate dates against major religious and national holidays; see master-timeline. Done: the dates are cleared or the conflict is recorded.

  4. Set the slot template: session length, changeover, breaks and lunch. Done: a one-page timing template.

  5. List rooms with capacity, projection and recording capability, and assign sizes using attendee interest data. Done: a room list, with the expectation that slots will move, because interest data keeps changing (L-3039; S026).

Building the grid#

  1. Place keynotes and the fixed sessions first. Done: the draft grid shows them.

  2. Place accepted sessions to balance ability levels, keep the best-known presenters apart and fit rooms. Done: a full draft grid.

  3. Spread panels and other heavy formats across the days. Done: no day carries most of one format.

  4. Have a second person who knows every session check pacing and sequence. Done: the second reader has signed off.

Publishing and changes#

  1. Circulate the draft to speakers with a reply date. Done: replies are logged.

  2. Publish the grid and link the grid view from the home page. Done: the public page matches the final draft.

  3. Set a date after which a slot move needs the program lead. Done: the rule is written down and shared with speakers.

Principles and general lessons#

Three principles have passed the promotion rule on structural reasons. Five further pieces of advice rest on one conference each and are tracked here until independent evidence arrives:

  • Run three to five parallel sessions rather than two, with at least three tracks loosely defined by skill level (L-1078, L-1080; S009).

  • Put longer sessions in the morning and keep the afternoon fast-paced (L-1085; S009).

  • Do not require advance signup to attend sessions (L-1086; S009).

  • Give lightning talks three to four minutes rather than seven, and allow one hour per session rather than 45 minutes (L-2099, L-2100; S036).

  • Pay for a hosted scheduling service rather than building one (L-3035; S026).

[general]

lesson | scope: general | source: S009, S087 | ledger: L-1081, L-1083 | confidence: observed | status: active | basis: structural | reason: Two sessions held at the same time cannot both be attended, so overlap costs attendance at both.

End each day with lightning talks and birds-of-a-feather sessions, in consecutive slots and never at the same time.

[general]

lesson | scope: general | source: S009, S036 | ledger: L-1082, L-2097, L-2098 | confidence: observed | status: active | basis: structural | reason: Moving between rooms and talking with people takes physical time that depends on the venue layout, not on the year.

Leave at least five minutes between sessions, more where rooms are far apart, and make the main breaks 30 minutes rather than 15.

[general]

lesson | scope: general | source: S087 | ledger: L-1087 | confidence: observed | status: active | basis: structural | reason: Travel and leave are booked months ahead and are costly to change, whatever the year.

Publish the full agenda months before the conference, so attendees can plan travel around the days that matter to them.

Risks#

The annual meeting slot is too short or lacks remote access

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Obtain the Foundation's expectations on duration and remote access in writing before the grid freezes and reserve the slot first | owner-role: program lead

Judgment holds for a typical mid-size conference.

A schedule change after publication breaks speaker travel plans

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Circulate a draft to speakers, publish early and require program-lead approval for any slot move after publication | owner-role: program lead

A speaker withdraws after the grid is published

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Keep wait-listed sessions and a spare lightning-talk or open space slot that can absorb the gap | owner-role: program lead | playbook: speaker-dropout
