Venue#

Why it matters#

The venue sets the limits of the conference. It decides how many people can attend, how many tracks can run at once, where people eat and talk between sessions, and how much money is committed early. It is also hard to reverse, because the venue contract is usually the first large commitment the team signs.

A venue that fits badly shows up as long walks between buildings, rooms of the wrong size and a contract that penalizes the team when attendance falls short. A venue that fits well lets attendees stay in one place from the first training to the last sprint day.

Attendees, speakers, trainers, sprinters, sponsors, volunteers and the Foundation all depend on the choice. Food, lodging and the choice of city have their own chapters: Catering, Hotels and local logistics and City selection.

Applies when#

  • format=in-person A venue is needed only when people meet in person.

  • venue The advice varies with the type of venue: hotel, university, conference center or a mix.

  • size The number of rooms, the plenary capacity and the contract risk grow with attendance.

Decisions you must make#

  • OPTION Which kind of venue fits the conference: a university or public institution, a hotel or a conference center? The Foundation planning schedule notes that training, talk and sprint venues are often a university or a public institution (L-4029). Universities can offer lower cost, while a hotel puts lodging and food under one roof. (decide-by: T-12m)

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-4123, L-4131) Will the program run in one venue, or in buildings within a few minutes' walk of each other? If you must split, plan walking time, entry rules and food for each site before you sign. (decide-by: T-12m)

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-1022, L-2001, L-3085) Is the venue in a walkable district with lodging at several price levels and food and drink close by? Check it on foot. (decide-by: T-12m)

  • OPTION How many people must the plenary room and the parallel rooms hold? Past hosting calls asked for a plenary room for 200 to 500 people (250 to 500 in 2016 and 2017), breakout rooms for two or three tracks and informal gathering space (S049, S055, S059). Those figures are unconfirmed as of 2026-11, so size the rooms from your own ticket target and track count. (decide-by: T-12m)

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-1026, L-2071) Which rooms besides the talk rooms do you need, and for how many days? Confirm that the hangout, sprint and training rooms are free on every day from the first training to the last sprint day. (decide-by: T-9m)

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-1147, L-4029) Does the venue provide reliable wireless for all attendees at once, enough power and the audio-visual support you need? Past hosting calls asked for this, unconfirmed as of 2026-11. Get the capacity in writing. (decide-by: T-9m)

  • OPTION Will you accept a minimum-attendance or minimum-spend guarantee in the venue contract? Prefer none. If the venue insists, set any minimum below your break-even attendance and compare it with your ticket forecast. (decide-by: T-9m)

How-to#

Define and compare#

  1. Write a venue brief that lists the rooms, their capacity, the days, lodging, food, wireless, recording and accessibility needs. The Foundation planning schedule names food service, wireless and recording as venue concerns (L-4029). Done when the team has read the one-page brief.

  2. List candidate venues, including universities and public institutions where the team has a contact. Done when every candidate has a row with capacity, free dates and an indicative price.

  3. Ask each candidate for written confirmation that every room is free for the whole span. Done when each answer is on file.

  4. Visit the shortlist with the program lead and the audio-visual owner. Walk between rooms, count outlets, test the wireless and listen for noise. Done when each visit has a short report.

  5. Ask every building for its entry rules, food and drink rules and opening hours. Done when the rules for each site are written down.

  6. Compare the shortlist by total cost, walkability and risk, not by rental fee alone. Done when the team has recorded the comparison and the reason for the choice.

Contract#

  1. Ask whether a nonprofit or community rate applies and what proof the venue needs. Done when the venue has answered in writing.

  2. Negotiate cancellation and relocation terms, deposit schedule, access times and any attendance or spend guarantee. Done when the team's contract reviewer has signed off on the terms.

  3. Keep one backup venue on the shortlist, with its price and contact on file. Done when the backup is named in the team's risk list.

Prepare the days#

  1. Plan power: count floor outlets, then budget for extension cords, power strips and tape, and book volunteer time to lay them. Done when the power plan lists every room.

  2. Walk through every room with venue staff two days before the first session. Done when the punch list from the walk-through is closed.

Principles and general lessons#

[general]

lesson | scope: general | source: S088 | ledger: L-1026 | confidence: observed | status: active | basis: structural | reason: Conversation and food noise carry into session rooms, so the separation has to be physical.

Give attendees a separate room with tables and chairs where they can eat, work on laptops and talk without disturbing sessions. Place a physical message board near the main area.

[general]

lesson | scope: general | source: S009 | ledger: L-1147 | confidence: observed | status: active | basis: structural | reason: Every attendee brings a laptop, and the number of outlets in a building is fixed before the team arrives.

Prefer a venue with many floor outlets. Otherwise budget for extension cords, power strips and tape, and for several hours of volunteer time to lay and tape them.

[general]

lesson | scope: general | source: S034 | ledger: L-3085 | confidence: observed | status: active | basis: structural | reason: Attendees pay for lodging at very different budgets, and the pattern of lodging around a site is fixed by its location.

Choose a location within walking distance of both expensive hotels and cheap lodging, including hostels, with plenty of food and bar options nearby.

Case notes#

[specific · 2026 · Maastricht · 0 y]

lesson | scope: specific | year: 2026 | place: Maastricht | org: Plone Conference 2026 organizing team | source: S110 | ledger: L-6029, L-6030, L-6032, L-6033 | confidence: reported | status: active

Eleven survey respondents wrote about the hotel-integrated venue, most praising sessions, rooms and catering under one roof; one rated its Wi-Fi, temperature and seating above earlier venues. The cost was social: the venue sat far from the town center, and the analysis recommends a venue reachable from where people sleep, with organized evenings.

Risks#

The venue cancels or becomes unavailable after the contract is signed

risk | likelihood: low | impact: high | mitigation: Negotiate cancellation and relocation terms before signing and keep one backup venue shortlisted with its price on file | owner-role: venue lead | playbook: venue-loss

The cost of rebooking falls on the team if the contract is silent.

The contract guarantees an attendance that ticket sales do not reach

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: high | mitigation: Avoid attendance guarantees where possible, otherwise set any minimum below the break-even attendance and review sales against it at fixed dates | owner-role: treasurer

A guaranteed minimum turns a shortfall in sales into a direct cost, and ticket purchases tend to arrive late.

The rooms do not match the program

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Fix the track count and the size of the largest room from the ticket target before the site visit and check both against the brief | owner-role: program lead

Too few parallel rooms limit the program, and an oversized plenary room looks empty.

Power and wireless fall short at peak use

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Count outlets and test wireless during the site visit, and budget for power strips and volunteer crew time | owner-role: audio-visual lead

Every attendee brings at least a laptop and a phone.
