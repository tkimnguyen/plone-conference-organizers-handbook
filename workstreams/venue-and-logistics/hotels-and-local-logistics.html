Hotels and local logistics#

Why it matters#

Most attendees travel to the conference and pay for their own lodging. They need to know where to sleep, how to get from the airport to the venue and where to eat, and they need to know it early enough to book at a fair price.

The organizers do not control the hotel market, but they can shape it. They can choose a venue with lodging close by, negotiate group rates, publish a vetted list with distances and keep that list accurate. A venue hotel with discounted rooms also keeps people on site in the evening, which turns hallways and bars into working space, as described in the case notes.

When this work is missed, attendees book far away or at high prices, find a published hotel sold out, or arrive without a plan for the last mile. The sections below follow the order in which the decisions arise. The venue itself is covered in Venue, and the choice of city in City selection.

Applies when#

  • format=in-person Lodging and local travel matter only for people who attend in person.

  • size The number of rooms to negotiate and the pressure on local hotel capacity grow with attendance.

Decisions you must make#

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-2105, L-3122, L-4177) Will you publish a hotel list with distances to the venue and several price tiers, and name an owner who keeps it current? Attendees rely on the list on the website, so an out-of-date entry sends them to a full hotel. (decide-by: T-6m)

  • OPTION Will you negotiate group rates, or leave attendees to the market? One team called every hotel in the city, and the hotels were reluctant because they hoped to raise prices during large events (L-2075). Another conference used the discounted rooms of its venue hotel (L-2003). A third promoted its venue hotel together with a discount for an airline group (L-4230). (decide-by: T-9m)

  • OPTION Will you reserve a dedicated lodging for attendees only? One conference reserved a floating hotel that became a social hub (L-2082). It needs a lodging that can be reserved whole and a price that suits attendee budgets. (decide-by: T-9m)

  • OPTION How far may the venue be from an international airport? Past hosting calls asked for at most one hour, with affordable transport so that attendees need no car (S049, S052, S055, S059, S096). The rule is unconfirmed as of 2026-11. One bid advised flying to a major hub and connecting (L-3122). (decide-by: T-12m)

  • OPTION Will you add local tourism and transport suggestions to the housing information? The Foundation schedule groups housing for trainers and attendees, travel information and local tourism suggestions in one planning step (L-4030). (decide-by: T-6m)

How-to#

Research#

  1. Map lodging within walking distance of the venue or a short transit ride, in at least three price tiers. Done when a table lists distance, travel time, an indicative rate with its date of check and the booking route for each hotel.

  2. Ask the venue whether it has rooms and whether it offers a block or a discount. Done when the answer is in writing.

  3. Check the city's event calendar for the conference dates. Large events raise prices and make hotels reluctant to discount. Done when any clash is noted and negotiation starts early.

  4. Contact other hotels for group rates when the venue cannot house attendees. Done when each reply is recorded with its terms and expiry date.

Publish#

  1. Publish a lodging page with the list, distances, tiers, booking links and any discount codes with expiry dates. Done when the page is linked from the venue and travel pages.

  2. Publish travel directions: nearest airports, rail, local transit and the route from the airport to the venue, each with a date of check. Done when a first-time visitor can plan the whole trip from the page.

  3. Publish housing and travel information together with local tourism suggestions. The Foundation schedule places this step in March (L-4030). Done when the page carries the date of the last check.

  4. Mention visa support on the same page where it applies. Done when the page links to the visa information the team publishes.

Maintain and run#

  1. Name an owner who checks availability every week in the last three months and marks sold-out hotels. Done when the owner records each check.

  2. Put each discount-code deadline in the team calendar and remind attendees before it expires. Done when reminders are sent at least a week ahead.

  3. Put the hotel list, a local map and transit information at the registration table. Done when attendees can find them without asking.

Risks#

Hotels fill or raise prices before attendees book

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Start rate negotiations before the dates are announced, check the city's event calendar and publish alternatives in several price tiers | owner-role: logistics lead

A large local event on the same dates can remove rooms and raise prices. Judged for a mid-size conference in a city with few hotels near the venue.

The published hotel list or a discount code is out of date

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: low | mitigation: Name an owner for the list and set calendar reminders for each code's expiry date | owner-role: logistics lead

Attendees book from the list and trust it.

Attendees cannot find a workable route from the airport to the venue

risk | likelihood: low | impact: medium | mitigation: Publish airport-to-venue routes with times and costs, and check them against real connections before publishing | owner-role: logistics lead

The risk grows when the nearest international airport is far from the venue.
