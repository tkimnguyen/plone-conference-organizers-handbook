Catering#

Why it matters#

People sit in sessions for long hours, and the breaks are where much of the conference happens. Coffee, water and food decide whether people are comfortable enough to talk, learn and stay for the last session.

Food is also a cost line, a safety matter for people with allergies and dietary rules, and a source of volunteer workload. The venue often controls the choice of caterer, the price and the rules, so catering is tied to the venue contract, described in Venue.

Handled badly, catering shows up as long queues, people who miss food or the start of a talk, thirsty or hungry attendees, and billing disputes with the venue. Handled well, nobody notices it. The decisions below come first, the steps follow in the how-to, and the evidence from earlier conferences is in the case notes.

Applies when#

  • format=in-person Food and drink service is needed only when people meet in person.

  • size Queues, caterer minimums and the choice between meals and nearby food all change with attendance.

Decisions you must make#

  • OPTION Will you serve full lunches, or rely on food within walking distance? One conference skipped meals and served breakfast, a snack and drinks because cheap food was close (L-1027). Another served free lunch because there were no lunch places near the convention center and talks ran close to lunchtime (L-3100). Past hosting calls listed snacks, coffee and lunch as expected (S059), which is unconfirmed as of 2026-11. Walk the area at lunch hour on a weekday before you decide. (decide-by: T-9m)

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-1027, L-2004, L-4206) Will coffee, tea and water be available throughout each conference day? Check that the venue deal covers every day, including the first. (decide-by: T-9m)

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-1028, L-2040) Where will food and coffee be served, and how will you keep queues out of session time? Serve in a neutral space of its own with room around the tables and more than one station. (decide-by: T-6m)

  • OPTION Will you choose the caterer from the venue's approved list, or bring your own? One team chose by bids from the venue's approved list (L-1027). Ask the venue early whether it allows outside caterers and what that costs. (decide-by: T-9m)

  • OPTION Will you collect dietary needs at registration and pass them to the caterer? One set of attendee comments asked for a label on each dish (L-1030). Decide who owns the list and the date it goes to the caterer. (decide-by: T-6m)

How-to#

Plan#

  1. Write a catering brief with the days, the meal types (breakfast, coffee breaks, lunch, drinks), the head-count range, the dietary categories and the budget per head. Done when the brief is agreed by the treasurer and the venue lead.

  2. Walk the surroundings at lunch hour. Done when you hold a list of food options with walking times and indicative prices, each with a date of check.

  3. Ask the venue what catering is bundled, exclusive or optional, and which caterers it approves. Done when the answer is in writing.

  4. Request bids from at least two caterers on the same brief. Done when the bids sit in one comparison sheet.

  5. Design the service: a space apart from the session rooms, several stations, and coffee and tea apart from the food line. Done when the plan marks the stations on the floor plan.

Contract#

  1. Put into the contract what is included on each day, the drinks, free water, dish labels, the date for final head counts and how late changes are charged. Done when the contract reviewer has signed off.

  2. Tell the venue in writing which items are included for attendees and must not be charged at the bar or table. Done when the venue manager has confirmed the list.

  3. Agree how dietary needs reach the caterer, and by which date. Done when both sides name the owner and the date.

On the days#

  1. Walk through each break with the caterer before the first session. Done when the caterer has the schedule and sets up before each break begins.

  2. Assign a volunteer to watch the queue and the refills at every break. Done when the volunteer reports the issues at the daily meeting.

  3. Check the venue's bill each day against the contract. Done when differences are raised the same day.

Principles and general lessons#

[general]

lesson | scope: general | source: S088 | ledger: L-1028 | confidence: observed | status: active | basis: structural | reason: Sound, smell and foot traffic from a food service disturb a session held in the same room.

Serve food in a neutral space of its own, not in a room where sessions run.

[general]

lesson | scope: general | source: S088 | ledger: L-1029 | confidence: observed | status: active | basis: structural | reason: One food line serves a fixed number of people a minute, so queue length follows from head count and break length.

Set up before the break, use several smaller stations and serve coffee and tea apart from the food line. Keep tables clear of walls so people can reach them from more than one side. Otherwise queues make people miss food or the start of talks.

[general]

lesson | scope: general | source: S088 | ledger: L-1030 | confidence: observed | status: active | basis: structural | reason: A label at the dish is the only place where a person can check their dietary needs while choosing.

Offer freely available water and fewer sugary and starchy items. Put a label on each dish instead of one menu at the far end of the table.

Risks#

Queues at breaks make attendees miss food or the start of sessions

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Serve in a separate space with several stations, set up before each break and have a volunteer watch the queue | owner-role: catering lead

Judged for a mid-size conference; the effect grows when every session ends at the same time.

A dietary need or allergy does not reach the caterer

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: high | mitigation: Collect dietary needs at registration, send them to the caterer by the agreed date and label every dish | owner-role: catering lead

A missed allergy is a safety event, not a service lapse.

Attendees are charged for items the conference included

risk | likelihood: low | impact: low | mitigation: Give venue staff the list of included items in writing and check the bill after the first day | owner-role: treasurer

The venue staff may not know the arrangement that the organizers made with the manager.

The final head count differs from the number given to the caterer

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Agree the date and the tolerance for final numbers in the contract and compare ticket sales against the number at fixed dates | owner-role: treasurer

Ticket purchases arrive late, so early numbers understate attendance.

No food is available nearby when the program runs through lunch

risk | likelihood: low | impact: medium | mitigation: Walk the area at lunch hour during the site visit and arrange food on site when nothing is within a short walk | owner-role: catering lead

The risk applies when the conference relies on nearby food instead of serving lunch.
