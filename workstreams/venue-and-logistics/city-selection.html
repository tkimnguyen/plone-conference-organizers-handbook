City selection#

Why it matters#

The host city decides who can afford to come, how long the trip takes and which rules apply to visitors. It also decides which local team carries the work, and which venues and hotels the conference can use. Every later decision, from the venue to the budget, starts from this one.

A city that is cheap to reach from the regions where attendees live widens the audience. A city that is hard or expensive to reach narrows it, and the team may learn that only when ticket sales stall. The choice also touches the Foundation, which approves the location and the use of the conference name, and the local community, which supplies volunteers and credibility.

The evidence in this chapter is thin in one respect: how the Foundation selects a host today is not documented in the archive. The decisions below say what the archive supports and what still needs confirmation. Venue supply is covered in Venue and lodging in Hotels and local logistics.

Applies when#

  • format=in-person The city matters when people travel to meet.

  • size The venue supply and the lodging capacity needed depend on attendance.

  • jurisdiction Visa rules, taxes and legal requirements depend on the country of the host city.

Decisions you must make#

  • MUST (req: R-001; as-of: 2026-11-02) Will the Foundation approve the location before you use the conference name? The Foundation selects the location, and the naming resolution requires its approval for "Plone Conference" and "International Plone Conference" (S006). Ask the Board before any public announcement. (decide-by: T-12m)

  • OPTION Who prepares and submits the hosting proposal, and to whom? Hosting calls from 2016 to 2020 asked for proposals to the Foundation Board, with Board pre-qualification and a vote by Foundation members (S049, S052, S055, S059, S096). No later call is archived, so confirm the process with the Board. (decide-by: T-12m)

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-1014, L-4087, L-4100) Is the city reachable at reasonable cost from the regions where most attendees live? A bid can argue from an airport near the center, low-cost flights and a rail link, as one conference did (L-4087). Another was chosen because it was reachable from both Western and Eastern Europe (L-4100). A distant location made the trip long and expensive for one group (L-1014). Past hosting calls asked for at most one hour from an international airport with affordable transport, which is unconfirmed as of 2026-11. (decide-by: T-12m)

  • OPTION Will you rotate the conference between regions? A different region drew first-time attendees in one case (L-1013) and widened reach in another (L-4100). One informal analysis found no significant difference in attendance between Europe and North America (L-3089), but the evidence is weak. Weigh rotation against the strength of the local team. (decide-by: T-12m)

  • OPTION Which season and dates? Past hosting calls asked for a conference between mid-September and mid-November that avoids major religious and national holidays (S049, S055, S059). The requirement is unconfirmed as of 2026-11. Check the city's event calendar before you fix dates. (decide-by: T-12m)

  • OPTION Does the city have a venue that fits your parallel tracks and keynote room? In one city only three venues had enough rooms for all parallel talks plus a main room for keynotes (L-2002). Check venue supply before you commit to the city. (decide-by: T-12m)

How-to#

Frame the search#

  1. Write down what the city must offer: reach, venue fit, walkable lodging and food, visa conditions and a local team. Done when the criteria are agreed and recorded.

  2. Confirm the Foundation's hosting process with the Board before you invest in a proposal. Done when the process, the form and the dates are named in writing.

Evaluate#

  1. List candidate cities with the local team behind each. Done when each candidate has a named contact group and a short pitch.

  2. Map travel from the main attendee regions to each candidate: nearest international airport, travel time, rail links and typical fares, each with a date of check. Done when each candidate has a one-page travel summary.

  3. Check venue supply against the venue brief. Done when each candidate has a list of venues that fit.

  4. Walk the district around the leading venues and note lodging tiers, food, shops and walking distances. Done when the notes cover at least three price tiers of lodging.

  5. Check visa requirements for the main attendee nationalities and the cost of visa support. Done when each candidate has a visa summary with sources.

  6. Check the dates against local events, holidays and the hotel market. Done when clashes are listed per candidate.

Decide and propose#

  1. Score the candidates against the criteria and record the reasoning, including the groups each choice leaves out. Done when a decision memo lists advantages and disadvantages for every candidate.

  2. Write the hosting proposal for the process you confirmed. Done when the Board has acknowledged receipt.

  3. Hold the public announcement until the Foundation has approved the location. Done when the approval is on file.

Principles and general lessons#

[general]

lesson | scope: general | source: S088 | ledger: L-1015 | confidence: observed | status: active | basis: structural | reason: The mix of lodging and food around a venue is fixed by its site, and attendees reach it on foot.

Choose a walkable neighborhood with varied food, shops and lodging close to the venue. Publish a pre-researched list of nearby hotels with walking distances, which saves attendees time.

[general]

lesson | scope: general | source: S035 | ledger: L-3089 | confidence: reported | status: active | basis: structural | reason: Six conferences cannot separate the host region from year-specific causes such as the economy or the program.

Do not assume that the host continent decides attendance. An informal analysis of six conferences found no statistically significant difference between European and North American attendance. Six data points are weak evidence either way.

Case notes#

[specific · 2010 · Bristol · 16 y] stale-prone

lesson | scope: specific | year: 2010 | place: Bristol | org: Netsight Internet Solutions | source: S015 | ledger: L-2002 | confidence: observed | status: active

Only three venues in the host city had enough rooms for all parallel talks plus a main room for up to 400 people for keynotes. That narrowed the venue choice sharply.

[specific · 2009 · Budapest · 17 y] stale-prone

lesson | scope: specific | year: 2009 | place: Budapest | org: Greenfinity | source: S013 | ledger: L-4100 | confidence: reported | status: active

The conference was the first held outside the United States and Western Europe. The city was chosen as reachable from both Western and Eastern Europe.

[specific · 2007 · Naples · 19 y] stale-prone

lesson | scope: specific | year: 2007 | place: Naples | org: Abstract Open Solutions | source: S010 | ledger: L-4087 | confidence: observed | status: active

The bid argued from cheap access: an airport two kilometers from the center, many low-cost flights, a high-speed rail link to Rome and three-star hotels (as of the 2006 bid).

[specific · 2006 · Seattle · 20 y] stale-prone

lesson | scope: specific | year: 2006 | place: Seattle | org: ONE/Northwest | source: S009 | ledger: L-1013 | confidence: observed | status: active

Holding the first conference in the western United States drew a strong regional user base, many from larger organizations, as first-time attendees. The city was cheaper than major United States and European cities.

[specific · 2006 · Seattle · 20 y] stale-prone

lesson | scope: specific | year: 2006 | place: Seattle | org: ONE/Northwest | source: S009 | ledger: L-1014 | confidence: observed | status: active

The location made the trip long and expensive for Europeans, and some people abroad hesitated to travel to the United States. Nobody who applied reported visa problems.

Risks#

The chosen city is too costly or too far for the attendees the conference wants

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: high | mitigation: Map travel from the main attendee regions with dated fares and times before the proposal and note which groups the choice leaves out | owner-role: bid lead

The effect shows in ticket sales, often too late to change the location.

No venue in the city fits the program

risk | likelihood: low | impact: high | mitigation: Check venue supply against the venue brief before choosing the city | owner-role: bid lead

A narrow supply of venues with enough parallel rooms leaves the team a choice of one.

Visa rules keep invited participants from attending

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: high | mitigation: Check visa rules for the main attendee nationalities before proposing and plan invitation letters from the day registration opens | owner-role: visa and travel owner

Rules differ by country and passport and change without notice.

The Foundation does not approve the location or the name

risk | likelihood: low | impact: high | mitigation: Confirm the approval process with the Board before any public announcement or binding commitment | owner-role: bid lead

The Foundation approves the location and the use of the conference name.
