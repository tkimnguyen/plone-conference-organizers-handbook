Hybrid and online#

Why it matters#

Some people cannot travel, and some conferences cannot meet in person. The Plone community has run fully online editions and in-person editions with remote access, and the experience differs a lot between them.

Remote access also costs effort and carries a worry. Organizers have feared that a live stream would keep people from traveling, while others see remote access as the only way for some community members to take part. The evidence on that point is thin, and this chapter says so.

Recording and publishing sessions are in AV, recording and internet. The footprint side of travel is in Sustainability.

Applies when#

  • format Applies to every conference, since an in-person team also decides about remote access, and the content varies by format.

Decisions you must make#

  • OPTION Which participation model do you offer: recordings only, live stream with chat, hybrid or fully online? Bristol declined live streaming and promised fast recordings (L-2015), Namur streamed with a chat channel and delayed recordings (L-4179), and the 2020 and 2021 editions ran fully online (L-4163, L-4168). (decide-by: T-9m)

  • OPTION How will streaming support remote access, and do you accept that it might affect in-person participation? The 2026 debrief raises substitution as a possibility but supplies no measured effect (L-5028). Make the choice explicit, whichever way it goes. (decide-by: T-9m)

  • OPTION How do remote attendees ask questions and meet people? Namur used a chat channel (L-4179). An organizer of both online editions said the platform could not recreate hallway encounters (L-4180). (decide-by: T-6m)

  • OPTION Do you offer trainings online? A 2024 survey called online training logistically challenging and asked for better integration (L-4202). The 2020 edition moved training to a weekend (L-4164). (decide-by: T-6m)

  • OPTION Which platform do you use, and who provides it? The 2021 edition used a platform from a sponsoring company (L-4168). (decide-by: T-4m)

  • OPTION Do you invite community members with their own equipment to cover extras? A Seattle volunteer recorded lightning talks and interviews that the professionals could not cover (L-1128). (decide-by: T-3m)

How-to#

  1. Write down the participation model and its reasons. Done: a one-page decision with an owner, shared with the program and AV leads.

  2. For a live stream, set up a question path for remote viewers, for example a chat channel watched by a volunteer (L-4179; S068). Done: a named person relays questions to the session chair.

  3. Plan fast publication of the recordings, so viewers outside the venue are not left waiting (L-2015; S015). Done: a publication date agreed with the recording owner.

  4. For training, test the online logistics before the date and decide how remote and in-room participants share exercises (L-4202; S074). Done: a trainer checklist covering audio, shared screens and breaks.

  5. For an online edition, choose the platform early and check whether a sponsor can provide it (L-4168; S064). Done: a written platform decision.

  6. Tell remote attendees up front what they will miss, since talks transfer well while hallway talk and sprint work do not (L-4178; S068). Done: a short note on the program page.

Principles and general lessons#

No general lesson has passed the promotion rule yet. One piece of advice rests on one discussion and is tracked until independent evidence arrives:

  • Community members who attended both formats say talks are much the same online, but remote attendees miss real-time questions in training, hallway conversations and the sprint (L-4178; S068).

Risks#

Streaming reduces in-person attendance

risk | likelihood: low | impact: medium | mitigation: State your position on streaming before registration opens and compare registrations with expectations as the date nears | owner-role: program lead

The evidence is a stated fear in 2010 and a 2026 debrief remark, with no measurement.

The format changes to online at short notice

risk | likelihood: low | impact: high | mitigation: Keep cancellation terms and a fallback date in the venue contract and decide who can call the switch | owner-role: conference chair | playbook: force-majeure

The 2020 edition moved online and ran three weeks later than first announced.

Remote attendees get a second-class experience

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Provide a question path, publish recordings quickly and say plainly what remote attendees will miss | owner-role: program lead

Remote attendees reported missing real-time questions, hallway talk and the sprint.

Online training logistics fail

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Test audio, shared screens and exercise handling with each trainer before the date | owner-role: training lead

A 2024 survey called online training logistically challenging.
