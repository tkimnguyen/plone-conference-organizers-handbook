Marketing and outreach#

Why it matters#

A conference sells nothing until the right people know it exists, see what is in it for them and can persuade their employer to pay. Marketing turns a venue, a program and a ticket price into attendance. When it is thin, the symptom is a sales curve that stays flat until the last weeks, when little can still be fixed.

The Foundation expects every hosting proposal to say how it will attract site builders who have not used Plone before. It also expects reach into the traditional segments (nonprofits, education, government, intranets) and into emerging corporate users (L-4072, S049). That makes marketing a plan with named audiences, not a general wish for visibility.

Marketing touches the Foundation, sponsors who expect reach, the Plone community, and other organizations whose audiences are not yours to use. Outreach to those organizations adds a duty that advertising does not have. A message nobody asked for costs trust, and a volunteer-run conference lives on trust.

This chapter feeds the low-ticket-sales playbook in Part 3, which starts from the audience plan written here. The website, email and brand mechanics sit in Web, comms and brand.

Applies when#

  • all Applies to every Plone conference, because every conference has to find its audience.

Decisions you must make#

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-1100, L-4072) Is there a written marketing plan that names the audience segments and one channel for each? Hosting calls ask for such a plan (requirements register row R-027, unverified as of 2026-11-02, so not a MUST here). Treat the established community, the traditional sectors and corporate users as separate segments. (decide-by: T-12m)

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-4109, L-5036) Who owns local audience development, or is it explicitly dropped from scope? Reaching local people is a scope decision, not a side effect of choosing a city. Name one owner and one dated first action, or record in the Tailoring Record that you are not pursuing it. (decide-by: T-9m)

  • OPTION Will you appear at other events, and as a speaker or as an exhibitor? A talk or case study at another sector's conference costs far less than sponsoring it, and booth traffic mostly came from people already known. (decide-by: T-9m)

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-1063, L-2012) Which headline sessions will you confirm and announce before the program closes? Early named sessions gave marketing something concrete to sell and allowed sector-specific invitations. (decide-by: T-6m)

  • OPTION Will you contact other organizations and communities directly? If yes, work permission-first, as in the how-to. Consent rules differ by country and by kind of recipient, so check the rules with a qualified local adviser before the first campaign. (decide-by: T-6m)

  • OPTION Will you offer attendees a letter template for asking their employer to approve the trip? One conference did, on its site. (decide-by: T-4m)

How-to#

Plan (T-12m to T-9m)#

  1. Write the audience plan on one page: three segments, one channel and one measurable outcome each. Done: the plan is filed with your Tailoring Record.

  2. Decide the local audience question. Done: a named owner and a first action with a date, or a recorded decision to drop it.

  3. Contact local Python groups to collaborate or to hold a joint event (L-4033, S004). Done: each group has answered, even if the answer is no.

  4. Create one outreach dataset with one row per organization, role and route, never one row per scraped person (L-5059, S102). Done: columns for owner, permission, outcome, next action and suppression exist.

  5. Regenerate every spreadsheet or print view from the dataset and never edit a view alone (L-5059). Done: the dataset is under version control and views carry a build date.

Prepare (T-9m to T-4m)#

  1. Confirm headline sessions and keynotes as early as the speakers allow. Done: at least three session titles you may publish.

  2. Prepare one one-page handout per product, for example the technical conference, a business track or trainings. Done: each handout is tagged with its product, and you send only the one the recipient asked about.

  3. Record a promo video, either produced or simply organizers explaining why people should come (L-4035, S004). Done: the video is linked from the website.

  4. Draft one covering message per route with one relevance sentence, one offer and one request. Done: a second person has read each draft.

Run (T-6m to T-1w)#

  1. Verify each organization's current work and its own contact route before you write (L-5057, S102). Done: the row is marked verified with the date.

  2. Recheck the matching session, ticket path and price before you attach any handout (L-5057). Done: the handout facts agree with the live site.

  3. Contact permission-first and record objections or suppression in the dataset at once (L-5056, S102). A public contact route does not by itself give permission to send a campaign. Done: every contact has a recorded permission basis.

  4. Ask for one permission at a time: an introduction, forwarding and access to an audience are separate, as the principle below says. Done: the dataset records which one was granted.

  5. Release routes in waves, then pause and read the replies before releasing the next wave. Done: the second wave contains only routes supported by replies, introductions or attributed registrations.

  6. Record the result and next action in the dataset after each permitted contact (L-5059). Done: no follow-up lives only in a mailbox.

  7. Measure outcomes, not reach: positive replies, completed introductions, activated advocates and attributed registrations. Done: a weekly count of those four, with list size and opens left out.

Principles and general lessons#

[general]

lesson | scope: general | source: S103 | ledger: L-5058 | confidence: observed | status: active | basis: structural | reason: Consent to one kind of contact does not extend to a different act, so each act needs its own permission.

Treat introductions, forwarding and access to another organization's audience as separate permissions. A relationship or a directory entry grants none of them automatically.

Two more pieces of advice rest on one account each and stay candidates until a second conference confirms them. An early organizer team advised considering a public relations committee with technology-sector marketing experience (L-1101). The same team advised seeking channels that reach technologists in higher education and in city and state government (L-1103).

Risks#

Planned local outreach is never executed

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Give local audience development one named owner and one dated first action, or record in the Tailoring Record that it is out of scope | owner-role: marketing lead | playbook: low-ticket-sales

Planning a local channel does not produce attendance until someone runs it.

Headline sessions are announced after sales have stalled

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: high | mitigation: Fix a date for confirming and publishing headline sessions before the program closes, and review the sales curve against it | owner-role: program chair | playbook: low-ticket-sales

Judged for a typical mid-size conference.

An outreach message reaches recipients who gave no permission

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: high | mitigation: Contact organizations only through verified organization-owned routes, record permission and suppression in the dataset, and have a qualified local adviser review the rules first | owner-role: outreach lead

A public contact route does not establish permission to send a campaign.
