Health, safety and emergencies#

Why it matters#

Hundreds of people spend several days in a venue they do not know, with cables, stairs, long hours and little sleep. Most days nothing happens. On the day something does, the team needs to know the local emergency number, who calls it, where a first-aid kit is and how the room is cleared.

The handbook's evidence here is thin, and this page says so. It records the few hazards reported and lists what is missing, so the next team does not assume someone else has planned for it.

Applies when#

  • format=in-person,hybrid Any conference with people in a room needs a safety plan, and the venue's own rules apply as well.

Decisions you must make#

  • OPTION Who is the named safety contact on site, and who is the backup? Choose a person who stays reachable during sessions and social events. (decide-by: T-3m)

  • OPTION Which emergency information does the team collect from the venue? At least the local emergency number, the evacuation routes and the location of first-aid equipment. (decide-by: T-6m)

  • OPTION How are cables managed in rooms and walkways? Route power cords along the edges and keep walkways clear. (decide-by: T-1m)

How-to#

  1. Ask the venue in writing for its emergency procedure, the evacuation routes and the location of first-aid equipment. Done when the answer is filed with the venue contract.

  2. Write down the local emergency number and the address of the nearest emergency medical service. Done when both are in the volunteer briefing.

  3. Name a safety contact and a backup, and put the route to them in the briefing for volunteers and staff.

  4. Walk each room with the venue contact before doors open and check cables, doors and stairs. Done when walkways are clear.

  5. Run power cords along the back edges of tables or under covers, away from where people walk.

  6. Keep water and simple snacks at the volunteer office and the registration desk.

Risks#

A participant needs medical help and nobody knows the procedure

risk | likelihood: low | impact: high | mitigation: Collect the local emergency number and first-aid locations before arrival and put them in the briefing for volunteers | owner-role: safety contact

Delay in the first minutes is the harm.

A cable or step in a walkway causes a fall

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: low | mitigation: Walk each room before doors open and route cords along edges or under covers | owner-role: venue liaison

An attendee reported cords as a tripping hazard at one conference (L-1148 is hearsay).
