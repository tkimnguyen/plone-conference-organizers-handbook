Social events and party#

Why it matters#

Many attendees say the evenings are where the community really forms. A first-time attendee described the party as the place where people get to know each other and move issues forward (S079, as of 2025). A conference that leaves the evenings open can leave newcomers on their own while insiders gather elsewhere.

The party is also the largest optional cost and a source of rules. The Foundation limits what its funds may pay for, a venue can fail a permit days before the date, and the way the party is paid for changes the ticket price.

The evening sits beside the day program, see scheduling. Wayfinding and the first-day routines are in Signage and showtime.

Applies when#

  • all Every conference has evenings to fill, with or without a party.

Decisions you must make#

  • MUST (req: R-031; as-of: 2026-11-02) If Foundation funds pay for any part of the event, does the budget keep them away from alcohol for resale? The register says alcohol paid from Foundation funds is only for consumption with meals. Split party costs into lines that make this visible. (decide-by: T-9m)

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-1109, L-4207, L-4220) Is there at least one structured evening event open to everyone? One team recommended it, a 2024 survey asked to reinstate the party, and a 2025 attendee valued it. (decide-by: T-9m)

  • OPTION Who pays: the registration fee, a separate ticket or the attendee? Bristol put the party price in the fee (L-2060), Budapest made it free for attendees with guest tickets on sale (L-4104), Ferrara sold a separate plus-one ticket (L-4160), and Brasília paid through party tickets with only the venue from the budget (L-3101). (decide-by: T-6m)

  • OPTION What does the party venue need to offer? One list asked for closeness to the conference, room for 400, local caterers, own drinks and late opening (L-2017). One party was held outdoors (L-4195), and one lost its location to a missing fire permit (L-3076). (decide-by: T-6m)

  • OPTION How loud is the music, and how local is the program? Bristol kept a live band at moderate volume (L-2043) and served regional food and ale (L-2042). (decide-by: T-4m)

  • OPTION Is there an arrival-eve event? A jetlag reception drew 40 to 50 people (L-2048), and an early-birds' dinner gave early arrivals a first occasion (L-3125). (decide-by: T-4m)

  • OPTION How do you guide the free evenings? Seattle suggested a neighborhood each night, which attendees liked (L-1108, L-1112), and offered no dinner at all (L-1107). One bid planned evening talks and concerts (L-4092). (decide-by: T-3m)

How-to#

Before booking#

  1. Decide who pays for the party and in which line it sits, and record it in the budget. Done: a budget line per cost, party separate from the venue booking.

  2. Check the Foundation rule: Foundation funds may not buy alcohol for resale, while alcohol for consumption with meals at Plone events is acceptable (L-4048; S005, as of 2026-11). Done: the treasurer confirms in writing which costs the Foundation may cover.

  3. List venue criteria: distance, capacity, caterer and drink rules, closing time. Done: a one-page criteria sheet.

Before the party#

  1. Shortlist venues against the criteria and confirm permits in writing with the venue, well before the date. Done: a signed contract and a permit confirmation.

  2. Publish the ticket and guest policy with the other tickets. Done: a public line on the tickets page.

  3. Write a short neighborhood guide for each free evening with food and drink options. Done: one page per evening, posted before the first day.

On the night#

  1. Check volume at the start, so conversation stays possible. Done: a named person owns the dial.

  2. Use the event for community awards and recognition. Done: a short slot in the run sheet.

Risks#

The party venue or permit fails days before the date

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: high | mitigation: Confirm permits and capacity in writing with the venue and keep one backup venue shortlisted | owner-role: social lead | playbook: venue-loss

One vendor could not obtain a fire permit three days before the party.

Weather forces an outdoor party indoors

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: low | mitigation: Book an indoor fallback space or a covered area when the party is outdoors | owner-role: social lead | playbook: force-majeure

One conference held its party outdoors.

Unstructured evenings leave newcomers out

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Offer one structured evening open to everyone and a daily guide to places to eat | owner-role: social lead

Attendees reported evenings dominated by insiders when no event was organized.

A funding rule is broken by mistake

risk | likelihood: low | impact: medium | mitigation: Keep Foundation-funded costs and alcohol in separate budget lines and ask the treasurer before booking | owner-role: treasurer

The rule on alcohol for resale applies when Foundation money is involved.
