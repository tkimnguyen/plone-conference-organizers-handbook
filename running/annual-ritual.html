The annual ritual#

Who keeps the handbook up to date, and when? The team that organizes the next conference updates the handbook together with the team that just finished. The work takes about 120 days after the last conference day, and a scheduled workflow reminds everyone about stale pages all year.

The dates on this page count from the last conference day, T+0. They are proposed, as part of the governance proposal in Governance, and the community may change them.

Why a ritual#

The origin problem of this project is that most conferences passed on little. A ritual makes the handover a fixed event with named deliverables, so that updating the handbook does not depend on anyone's spare time.

Who does what#

  • The incoming team leads the update.

  • The outgoing team delivers the handover, answers questions and reviews the lessons from its own year.

  • The maintainer team merges pull requests, runs the generator and cuts the release.

  • The stewards and deputies of the touched chapters review.

The handover deliverables#

The outgoing team owes these items by T+60 days. They are defined in the page handover-contract.

  1. Post-mortem, on the Seattle 2006 skeleton; the template is post-mortem-template.

  2. Financial summary: final income and costs by category, the result and the budget model, generalized.

  3. Anonymized survey data: aggregates only.

  4. Asset and credential handover: a list of accounts, domains and assets with their holders after the handover. Credentials pass through a password manager or the Foundation, never through the repository.

  5. Vendor contacts: organization-level, handed over privately. The public handbook lists none.

  6. The "expected but never written down" list: every implicit expectation the team ran into.

Two more items belong to the same contract.

  • The Tailoring Record, filed later in the sequence; see Tailor.

  • A shadowing arrangement: the incoming team follows the outgoing team's final months and the event itself.

The sequence#

By

Step

Done looks like

T+60 days

The six handover deliverables arrive.

Each item is delivered, or its absence is recorded with a reason.

T+75 days

The post-mortem and the anonymized survey results are filed under conferences/<year>-<city>/.

Both files exist in the handbook's docs/ tree and pass the privacy lint.

T+90 days

Lessons arrive as pull requests.

Every ledger row has a disposition, every lesson cites a ledger ID, and the promotion rule has been applied.

T+100 days

The outgoing team's Tailoring Record is filed as conferences/<year>-<city>/tailoring.md.

The record is merged. Recurring deviations are listed as candidates for a DEFAULT to become an OPTION.

T+110 days

The requirements register is re-verified and every MUST badge is re-dated.

The as-of date in each MUST badge equals that of its register row.

T+120 days

The release is cut.

as-of-year is bumped, the generator has run, help-wanted issues are refreshed, promotions are logged and the CalVer tag <year>.1 exists.

Step by step#

1. Collect the handover (by T+60 days)#

  1. The incoming team asks the outgoing team for the six deliverables in writing, with the date.

  2. The outgoing team delivers what it has and lists what is missing.

  3. A maintainer opens one tracking issue for the year and links each deliverable.

  4. Credentials and vendor contacts stay out of the repository.

2. File the records (by T+75 days)#

  1. Place the post-mortem and the anonymized survey aggregates under conferences/<year>-<city>/ in the docs/ tree.

  2. Check the files against the privacy rules: no personal data, no private amounts, no contact details.

  3. Open a pull request. The continuous integration run applies the privacy lint.

3. Turn lessons into pull requests (by T+90 days)#

  1. Add a ledger row for each lesson worth keeping, with a disposition: placed in a chapter, or dropped with a reason.

  2. Write the lessons as case notes, following Update.

  3. Apply the promotion rule: a specific lesson becomes general only with evidence from three independent conferences across two organizing bodies, or with a structural reason that does not depend on the year.

  4. Record each promotion as one line in the changelog.

  5. Review each lesson with a reviewer from a different organizing body than the one that ran that year.

4. File the Tailoring Record (by T+100 days)#

  1. The outgoing team copies its record into conferences/<year>-<city>/tailoring.md.

  2. The maintainer team compares it with earlier records and opens an issue for each DEFAULT that several conferences deviated from.

5. Re-verify MUST items (by T+110 days)#

  1. The incoming team re-checks the Foundation requirements register against the Foundation's own documents.

  2. It updates the as-of date of each verified row.

  3. It re-dates every MUST badge that cites a changed row. Until that is done, the badges fail the lint on purpose.

6. Cut the release (by T+120 days)#

  1. Bump as-of-year in the handbook configuration by hand. Lesson ages follow from it.

  2. Run the generator in write mode, then in check mode; see Update.

  3. Refresh the help-wanted issues from the thin registry; see About the thin registry.

  4. Tag the release with a CalVer tag: 2026.1, then 2027.1 and each year after. There is one living main branch and no branch per year.

  5. Report the bus-factor figure in the release notes.

The stale-page workflow all year#

A scheduled workflow runs monthly. It lists every written page whose review-by date has passed and opens one issue per page, naming the steward and the deputy. If a page already has an open freshness issue, it adds no second one. Closing an issue takes a commit that moves review-by forward, which is the act of re-confirming the page.

The workflow lives in .github/workflows/stale-pages.yml. It calls the freshness script, which belongs to the test harness. If the script is absent, the workflow only reports that fact and does not fail.

What the permanent record holds#

The living handbook changes every year. The conference records under conferences/<year>-<city>/ do not. They hold the post-mortem, the numbers, the Tailoring Record and source links for one year, and they are added to, never overwritten. A tag such as 2026.1 is a citable snapshot of the handbook as it stood after that year's ritual.
