# Annex: small-budget variant

This annex is general information, not legal, tax or insurance advice.
Check current rules with a qualified local adviser.

## What this annex is for

This annex is for teams that expect a small budget, few paid staff or a small audience.
It is a stub. The handbook's evidence cannot yet say what works at that scale, and this page says so before it offers anything.

## Applies when

- `size=small` The conference expects up to 150 attendees.
- `budget` The team has little or no cash reserve and depends on a seed loan, a financial agent or its own risk.

## Read this first: survivorship bias

Every conference in the archive happened, and most were carried by a company, a university or an established association with staff and a Foundation relationship.
The archive holds no account of a bid that failed or a conference that was cancelled for lack of money, as of 2026-11.
So the evidence base shows what organizers with resources reported, plus a few stories of strain.
A lever below worked for the team that reported it. It is not shown to work for a team without that backing.
Use the levers as questions to test, not as a recipe.

## Levers organizers reported

### Low-cost venue and food

Seattle 2006 used a small, affordable, city-owned conference facility in a walkable district (L-1022, S009).
Because cheap food was within walking distance, the team skipped full meals and served breakfast, a snack and drinks (L-1027, S009).
It also did not organize a dinner for hundreds of people, as novice organizers with an uncertain budget (L-1107, S009).
The city itself was cheaper than the major cities of the day (L-1013, S009).
See [venue](../../workstreams/venue-and-logistics/venue.md) and [catering](../../workstreams/venue-and-logistics/catering.md).

### Volunteer-heavy teams

The Seattle conference was run by two staff of the host organization with helpers, and took over 350 staff hours (L-1006, S009).
The 2011 team reported that volunteers cost twice what it planned, in food and shirts, and judged it worth it (L-3023, S025).
A volunteer-heavy model moves cost from invoices to hours and thank-you items, and it does not remove cost.
See [volunteers and roles](../../workstreams/people/volunteers-and-roles.md).

### Co-location as cost sharing

The Brasília 2013 team merged with the national Python conference to cut costs (L-3092, S093), with bundled sponsorship packages (L-3112, S094) and one registration for both events (L-3131, S042).
The 2025 conference in Jyväskylä shared a host university with a national Python conference and gave it one day of the talk program (L-4215, L-4216, S079).
Co-location also shares the talk slots, so check what the main conference gives up.

### In-kind support

Organizers reported hosts and public bodies covering things a budget would otherwise pay for.
A local research institute hosted the 2006 sprint, a small sponsorship covered security and coffee, and a company paid for pizza (L-1153, S009).
In 2013 the regional government gave a discount on the venue (L-3093, S093).
In 2023 a summer-university body provided training space (L-4194, S071), and the hosts of the sprint provided refreshments (L-4199, S072).
In the United States, a nonprofit umbrella reportedly cut venue costs (see the [United States annex](../jurisdictions/us.md)).
Agree in-kind support in writing, with its value and what happens if it is withdrawn.

### Sponsorship carries the weight

In 2012 sponsors played the most important role in meeting the budget (L-2083, S036).
In 2013 most income came from sponsorship, not from registrations (L-3098, S093).
A small conference may find fewer sponsors, and no evidence here covers that case.
See [sponsors](../../workstreams/money-and-law/sponsors.md).

## Risks

- Early costs fall on the organizers personally. Likelihood high, impact high. In 2011 the lead organizer carried a very large personal credit-card balance (L-3008, S023). In 2013 loans from friends paid early costs until sponsorship arrived (L-3096, S093). Both are risk stories, and neither is a recommendation. Agree a seed loan or an agent's advance first, as in [finance](../../workstreams/money-and-law/finance.md).
- Attendance falls short of the break-even point. Likelihood medium, impact high. Bristol 2010 budgeted a break-even of 300 and drew 285 (L-2020, L-2021, S016). Set a lower cost floor and cut optional lines first.
- A small surplus covers outside costs but not organizers' time. Likelihood high, impact medium. Bristol 2010 reported this (L-2029, S016). Decide at the start whether volunteer hours are a gift.
- In-kind support is withdrawn late. Likelihood medium, impact medium. Keep a priced fallback for each in-kind item.
