# Annex: United States

This annex is general information, not legal, tax or insurance advice.
Check current rules with a qualified local adviser.

## What this annex is for

This annex collects what organizers of United States conferences reported about local paperwork and nonprofit status.
It is a stub. The evidence is two conference accounts, dated 2006 and 2011, and both are old.
Read each point as a report from organizers at that time, then verify it locally before you rely on it.

## Applies when

- `jurisdiction=US` The conference takes place in the United States, or a United States entity pays service providers or rents the venue.

## What organizers reported

### Paying service providers

The 2011 team in San Francisco reported that it needed a W-9 tax form from everyone who performed a paid service before it could pay them (L-3009, S023).
Collecting the forms was slow, partly because some providers had no computer or printer.
The organizers advised scheduling time for it.
Verify with a local adviser which forms your own payments need.

### Nonprofit umbrella and venue discounts

The same team reported that running the conference with or under a nonprofit umbrella could bring venue discounts of up to half and an exemption from sales tax (L-3059, S030).
They also reported that it took diligence: ask for the discount, and escalate when a vendor says there is none.
A related case note reports a university venue rented under nonprofit status that saved more than USD 20,000 as of 2011 (L-3060, S030), and the organizers judged the effort worthwhile when the budget is tight.
Whether your organizing entity qualifies depends on its own status, so check before you plan on the saving.
See the chapter on [venue](../../workstreams/venue-and-logistics/venue.md) for the general venue decisions.

### Dated context from Seattle

Seattle 2006 gives the one other United States account in the archive.
The host could not advance early costs, and the Plone Foundation lent USD 8,000 as of 2006 (L-1031, S009).
The public budget flagged about USD 30,000 of USD 39,000 in minimal expenses as due up front as of 2006 (L-1032, S090).
These are historical figures for context, not benchmarks.
The money-side reading is in [finance](../../workstreams/money-and-law/finance.md).

### Visas and invitation letters

Invitation-letter mechanics for visitors to the United States are not recorded here.
The general method is in [visas and travel](../../workstreams/people/visas-and-travel.md).

## Risks

- A venue discount is assumed in the budget and then refused. Likelihood medium, impact medium. Ask in writing early and keep a budget line without the discount.
- Payment to a provider is blocked because the tax form is missing. Likelihood medium, impact low. Start collecting forms when the contract is agreed, not when the invoice arrives.
- The organizing entity does not qualify for nonprofit treatment, so the planned saving does not exist. Likelihood medium, impact medium. Confirm the entity's status with a local adviser before pricing.
