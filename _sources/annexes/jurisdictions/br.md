# Annex: Brazil

This annex is general information, not legal, tax or insurance advice.
Check current rules with a qualified local adviser.

## What this annex is for

This annex collects what the Brasília 2013 organizers reported about running a conference in Brazil.
It is a stub built from one conference, run together with the national Python conference.
Read each point as a report from organizers in 2013, then verify it locally before you rely on it.

## Applies when

- `jurisdiction=BR` The conference takes place in Brazil, or Brazilian public bodies or associations pay, host or back it.

## What organizers reported

### Government bodies pay after the event

Brazilian government organizations paid a higher registration fee in 2013 (L-3095, S093).
The organizers explained that public payment commitments are settled only after the event.
They also involve taxes, extra costs for issuing a service invoice (`nota fiscal`) and work proving attendance for government participants.
If government bodies are a target audience, plan the invoice and attendance-proof work, and plan for cash arriving late.
Verify the current invoicing rules with a local accountant.

### An association as legal and financial backing

The volunteer group behind the bid had no legal entity of its own.
The national Python association, a nonprofit, provided financial and legal support in Brazil (L-3116, S094).
The general question of who contracts and who holds the money is in [legal entity and financial agent](../../job/legal-entity-and-financial-agent.md).

### Co-location with the national Python conference

The Plone bid first stood alone and later merged with the national Python conference to cut costs (L-3092, S093).
The bid named four reasons: reach a broader audience, lower operating costs for both events, better sponsorship packages and shared fun (L-3112, S094).
One registration was valid for both events (L-3131, S042).
Because it was two international nonprofit conferences, the organizers also won support from the regional government, including a venue discount (L-3093, S093).

### Portuguese-language support

Most local developers were not fluent in English.
The bid planned a Portuguese-speaking assistant in every English-language training, one Portuguese-only track, and one English track with translation into Portuguese (L-3120, S094).
See [accessibility and inclusion](../../workstreams/people/accessibility-and-inclusion.md) for the general language-access decisions.

### Cash at the start

The association had no cash at the start of 2013, and loans from friends paid early costs until sponsorship arrived (L-3096, S093).
Treat this as a risk story. The finance chapter records it under `finance#case-notes`.

## Risks

- Government registrations are priced like private ones and the invoice and proof-of-attendance work is not budgeted. Likelihood medium, impact medium. Price that work in, as the 2013 organizers did.
- Payments from public bodies arrive after the event and leave a cash gap. Likelihood high, impact medium. Fund early costs from sponsorship or an agreed advance, not from personal credit.
- The partner association's goals differ from the Plone conference's. Likelihood low, impact medium. Write down who owns the money, the surplus and the decisions before the bid.
