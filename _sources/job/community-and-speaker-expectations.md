# Community and speaker expectations

## Why it matters

This chapter exists because of the handbook's origin problem: much of what a Plone conference is expected to provide was never written down.
A first-time organizer meets these expectations one at a time, usually after a promise has been made or a cost has been committed.
The three questions to ask about every expectation are whether the community assumes it, whether anyone regulates it and whether it is still unsettled.

The 2026 debrief named conference-funded trainer lodging, on site or close by, as an implicit expectation and the largest financial risk of the edition (S098, as of 2026-11).
Compensation for trainers and speakers is not regulated, so organizers pay regardless of how long a training lasts.
The debrief also described a party as something nobody is entitled to and the community values, and it reported that speakers expect a reduced ticket and make up a large minority of participants, a ratio that still needs verification.

None of this is Foundation policy.
The records show the same questions arriving again and again, in a 2011 assumptions page, in Foundation board minutes of 2015, in a 2023 request for guidelines and in the 2026 debrief, each time without a rule to settle them.
This chapter names the expectations, marks what is settled and what is not, and shows where the money sits.

## Applies when

- `all` Every conference has trainers, speakers and a community with assumptions about both.

## Decisions you must make

- {bdg-primary}`DEFAULT` (basis: L-3067, L-5019) **Have you written down the expectations of trainers, speakers and the community before announcing anything, and shown the list to experienced members?** Unwritten assumptions create obligations a first-time organizer never budgeted for. (decide-by: T-14m)
- {bdg-primary}`DEFAULT` (basis: L-4115, L-4189, L-5021) **What does a trainer receive, and is it agreed in writing before the training offer is announced?** Compensation is unregulated, and costs arise regardless of session length, so confirm fee, ticket and travel terms explicitly. See `trainings`. (decide-by: T-12m)
- {bdg-primary}`DEFAULT` (basis: L-4115, L-5020) **Will the conference pay trainer lodging, and will it be on site or close by?** Settle the commitment, the location and a cap before you confirm the training offer. (decide-by: T-12m)
- {bdg-primary}`DEFAULT` (basis: L-4144, L-5023) **Do speakers get a reduced ticket, and is that tier in the pricing model from the start?** A contributor-heavy audience can make the discounted tier a substantial share of registrations. See `ticketing-and-registration`. (decide-by: T-10m)
- {bdg-secondary}`OPTION` **Will you host a party, and how large will it be?** Treat it as a valued community choice that the organizers decide, because the 2026 debrief describes no entitlement to a party (L-5022). (decide-by: T-10m)
- {bdg-secondary}`OPTION` **Will a State of Plone talk run as a keynote, and who gives it?** It is a community tradition that it is a keynote, and there is no tradition about who gives it (L-3066). (decide-by: T-8m)
- {bdg-secondary}`OPTION` **Will you invite Google Summer of Code students to the conference?** The planning schedule expects coordination with the program (L-4032, S004). Visa costs make an early approval risky (L-4185). (decide-by: T-6m)

## How-to

### Write the list

1. Open the expectations list in the Tailoring Record at the start of planning, with one line per audience: trainers, speakers, sponsors, attendees, volunteers and students.
   Done looks like a list that every team member can find.
1. Sort each expectation into expected, regulated or unsettled with the table below.
   Done looks like one state per line and a named source for each regulated item.
1. Decide every unsettled item and write the decision in the list before you announce anything.
   Done looks like a dated decision with an owner.
1. Show the list to experienced community members and to the Conference Committee, and change it if they object.
   Done looks like a recorded answer from at least two of them.
1. Publish the terms you chose in the call for trainers and the call for speakers.
   Done looks like terms that appear in the call text.

| Expectation | State | What the record shows |
|---|---|---|
| Trainer lodging paid and on site or close | Expected, not regulated | The 2026 debrief; a 2015 conference reimbursed trainer hotels. |
| Trainer fee | Unsettled | A 2015 board discussion of trainer incentive had no recorded outcome; a 2023 organizer asked for guidelines. |
| Free trainer ticket | Practice | A 2015 conference gave trainers one. |
| Reduced speaker ticket | Expected, not regulated | A 2018 conference let speakers buy at the early-bird price; lightning talks did not qualify. |
| Party | Valued, no entitlement | The 2026 debrief. |
| State of Plone keynote | Tradition | A 2011 assumptions page. |
| Foundation guidance on any of these | None located | Searched as of 2026-11. |

### Price the expectations in

1. Add trainer lodging, trainer terms and the speaker tier as budget lines, using `finance`.
   Done looks like three lines with an owner and a cap.
1. Track the share of speakers among registrations every month once sales open.
   Done looks like a monthly figure; the 2026 estimate of about 40% was never verified.
1. Name a stand-in for each promise that depends on one person.
   Done looks like a deputy in the list for every promise.

### Students

1. Coordinate with the Plone Google Summer of Code program as the planning schedule expects (L-4032, S004).
   Done looks like a list of invited students or a recorded decision not to invite them.

## Principles and general lessons

```{note} [general]
:class: hb-lesson hb-general
lesson | scope: general | source: S032, S046, S070, S098 | ledger: L-3065, L-3067, L-4116, L-4189, L-5019 | confidence: reported | status: active | basis: independent-conferences

Unwritten expectations about trainers and processes turn into disputes or unbudgeted costs.
Write down what you offer, before you announce it, and show the list to experienced community members.
```

Three further points come from one conference or two and are not promoted.
The State of Plone talk is a keynote by tradition with no tradition about the speaker, so treat both with care (L-3066, S032).
If you decide on the thought that people give up vacation time to come, the rest tends to resolve itself (L-3086, S034).
Include expected speaker discounts in the pricing model from the start (L-5023).

## Risks

```{warning} Trainer lodging commitment exceeds the budget
:class: hb-risk hb-likelihood-medium hb-impact-high
risk | likelihood: medium | impact: high | mitigation: Settle the lodging commitment, location and cap in writing before you confirm the training offer | owner-role: treasurer | playbook: cash-flow-crunch

The 2026 debrief calls conference-funded trainer lodging on site or nearby the largest financial risk (L-5020, S098).
The judgment is for a typical mid-size conference.
```

```{warning} Speaker and trainer discounts erode ticket revenue
:class: hb-risk hb-likelihood-medium hb-impact-medium
risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Model the discounted tier as a share of registrations from the start and check the real share monthly | owner-role: treasurer | playbook: low-ticket-sales

A contributor-heavy audience can make the discounted tier a large part of sales.
```

```{warning} Informal promises to speakers vanish with one person
:class: hb-risk hb-likelihood-medium hb-impact-medium
risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Record every promise to trainers and speakers in the shared expectations list with a deputy | owner-role: program lead | playbook: key-person-loss

A promise that lives in one inbox is lost when that person leaves, and the next team meets the obligation unprepared.
```
