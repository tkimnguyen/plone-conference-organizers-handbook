# Visas and travel

## Why it matters

A participant who cannot get a visa cannot attend, and the visa decision is made months before the conference by an authority the organizers do not control.
What the organizers control is information and paperwork: who needs a visa, what the application requires from the host, and how quickly the host can supply it.

Late or missing letters cost speakers, trainers and sprinters, and sometimes the contributors the conference most wants.
A host country's rules differ, change without notice and depend on the participant's passport, so this chapter gives a method and not a rule set.
Any statement about a specific country belongs in a jurisdiction annex, with its own date.

## Applies when

- `jurisdiction` The visa rules and the practical support depend entirely on the host country and the participants' nationalities.
- `format=in-person` Only in-person participation needs a visa for travel.

## Decisions you must make

- {bdg-primary}`DEFAULT` (basis: L-1105, L-3121, L-4153, L-4229) **Will you offer an invitation letter to every registered participant who needs one?** Confirm the registration and the dates in the letter, and keep a download or request route open all year. (decide-by: T-9m)
- {bdg-primary}`DEFAULT` (basis: L-1105, L-3121, L-4153) **Will you publish visa information on the conference website?** List which visitors need a visa or are exempt, based on the host country's own sources, and link to them. (decide-by: T-9m)
- {bdg-secondary}`OPTION` **Will you align visa timing with a program that has a late decision date?** One conference found that students of a summer program were hard to place in time, because the outcome is known around June while some countries need applications by early June (L-4184). (decide-by: T-9m)

## How-to

1. Treat invitation letters and visa support as a task for the whole planning year, not a step before the conference. Done when the task has an owner and a recurring entry in the team's calendar (L-4031).
1. Look up which nationalities need a visa for the host country and which are exempt, using the government's own pages. Done when the list names its source and its date of check.
1. Publish a visa information page that gives the list, the dates by which applications should start and how to request an invitation letter. Done when the page is linked from the venue and travel pages.
1. Prepare an invitation letter template that confirms the person's registration and the conference dates, on the organizer's letterhead. Done when a test letter has been checked by a person who has applied for a visa for that country.
1. Offer the letter as a download or on request after registration is confirmed. Done when a participant can obtain it without a manual exchange.
1. Tell speakers and trainers about the letter when they are accepted, before the program is public. Done when the acceptance message names the letter and the lead time.
1. Review the page and the letter text against the host country's rules at T-6m and again at T-3m, and update the date of the last check.

## Risks

```{warning} The invitation letter arrives after the application deadline
:class: hb-risk hb-likelihood-medium hb-impact-high
risk | likelihood: medium | impact: high | mitigation: Keep the letter available for download from the day registration opens and warn speakers about lead times when they are accepted | owner-role: registration lead

Applications may need to start months before the conference, and a participant who learns about the letter late may miss the date.
Judged for a mid-size conference.
```

```{warning} The visa page states a rule that has changed
:class: hb-risk hb-likelihood-medium hb-impact-medium
risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Link to the government source, publish the date of the last check and re-check at T-6m and T-3m | owner-role: visa and travel owner

Rules differ by country and passport and change without notice.
```

```{warning} Letters expose personal data of participants
:class: hb-risk hb-likelihood-low hb-impact-medium
risk | likelihood: low | impact: medium | mitigation: Keep the request log in the team's private files and share only what the letter needs | owner-role: privacy lead

Letters carry names, passport details and dates, which are personal data under privacy law.
```
