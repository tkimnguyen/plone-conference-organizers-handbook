# Volunteers and roles

## Why it matters

A Plone conference runs on unpaid people.
Registration, rooms, radios, lost property and the late-evening problems all depend on volunteers who also want to attend the conference.
If the team treats them as free labor, they burn out, drop out or leave the work to the two or three people who never stop.

The second risk is the task nobody owns.
Major roles get a name early.
The small mandates, such as the venue liaison or the cash box, tend to be discovered on the first day.
A written map of roles makes those gaps visible while there is still time to fill them.

This chapter covers volunteer staffing, care and thanks, and the role map that hands work from one person to the next.
Incident handling by the response team is in [Code of Conduct operations](code-of-conduct-operations.md).

## Applies when

- `all` Every Plone conference has helpers, whether the organizing body is a company, a volunteer group or the Foundation.

## Decisions you must make

- {bdg-primary}`DEFAULT` (basis: L-2102, L-3029) **Who is the named person in charge of each room and of the registration desk, for the whole conference?** Staff at least two dedicated people per room or track and at the registration desk, and keep an organizer in every room at all times. (decide-by: T-3m)
- {bdg-primary}`DEFAULT` (basis: L-3027, L-5052) **What do volunteers receive, and how do they get to attend sessions?** Offer a free ticket and, where the budget allows, lodging, and rotate shifts so helpers can attend the conference they support. (decide-by: T-6m)
- {bdg-primary}`DEFAULT` (basis: L-2091, L-3026) **How do room staff call for help?** Equip room staff with good radios, hands-free where possible, so a projector, Wi-Fi or water problem reaches someone in minutes. (decide-by: T-2m)
- {bdg-secondary}`OPTION` **Will you write a day-in-the-life narrative for every role?** One conference used this to expose handoffs and unowned tasks before arrival (L-5050, L-5055). It rests on a single conference, so adopt it by judgment. (decide-by: T-3m)
- {bdg-secondary}`OPTION` **Will you provide a volunteer room?** One conference called its volunteer office the team's lifeline (L-3031, L-3087). Both accounts come from the same conference. (decide-by: T-3m)
- {bdg-secondary}`OPTION` **How will you feed and thank volunteers, and will you hold a thank-you reserve?** Meals, a volunteer dinner and a small reserve for people who go far beyond their role are reported by one conference (L-3024, L-3028). (decide-by: T-3m)

## How-to

### Before the conference

1. Write one day-in-the-life narrative per role, and have each one reviewed alongside the narratives of adjacent roles. Done when every handoff between two roles has been read by both sides (L-5050).
1. Add the smaller mandates to the responsibility map: venue liaison, materials, on-site money, newcomer welcome and post-event wrap-up. Done when each has a name next to it (L-5055).
1. Keep a short role map that lists every role and every mandate still without an owner, and hand each person the narrative for their role. Done when the map has no empty owner field at T-2w.
1. Staff two volunteers per room or track and two at the registration desk, dedicated for the whole conference. Plan for half of them to be available beforehand for loading and setup (L-3029).
1. Plan extra helpers for opening day beyond the regular volunteers. People who want to build business contacts are good candidates for the registration desk (L-3033).
1. Decide what volunteers receive and put it in the invitation. Done when each volunteer knows before arrival what they get (L-3027).
1. Plan breakfast and lunch every day for volunteers and at least one special dinner. Not everyone will come to each meal (L-3024).
1. Order a visual marker that makes volunteers recognizable to attendees, such as distinctive shirts or a playful accessory that helpers are happy to wear (L-3025).
1. Buy or rent good radios, hands-free where possible (L-3026).
1. Reserve a room for supplies and for volunteers to retreat to, stocked with refreshments, a printer and reliable internet (L-3031).
1. Set aside a small thank-you reserve for helpers who go far beyond their role (L-3028).

### During the conference

1. Rotate shifts so volunteers can attend the sessions they helped to make possible. Where technical continuity needs a stable operator, agree on the exception out loud (L-5052).
1. Confirm each helper assignment with the person directly before the next day's shift. A published roster does not show that the person accepted the handoff (L-5053).
1. Check the combined workload of the person who owns daytime operations and any late social event. Done when a deputy holds one complete operational responsibility (see Risks).

### After the conference

1. Compare the public thank-you page with the full list of helpers before publishing it.
1. Hand the role map and the narratives to the next team as part of the handover.

Keep a named person responsible in every room at all times, coordinated by radio, and keep the lead who covers gaps free of routine shifts so there is attention left for whatever goes wrong.
```

Give volunteers a free ticket and lodging where possible, and rotate shifts so they can attend sessions.
Helping must not cost them the conference they came for.
```

```{note} [general]
:class: hb-lesson hb-general

If volunteers are not having fun, the organizers are doing something wrong.
Treat their experience of the work as a design requirement, not a bonus.
```

One conference reported that a fixed list assigning named tasks to named volunteers did not work on the day, and recommended good communication and flexible teamwork instead (L-3030).
That sits beside the role narratives in section 4, which assign mandates, not minutes.
Until a second conference confirms the finding, treat it as a single-source observation.

## Risks

```{warning} One person owns daytime operations and the late social event
:class: hb-risk hb-likelihood-medium hb-impact-high
risk | likelihood: medium | impact: high | mitigation: Delegate one complete operational responsibility to a deputy so the person who runs the late social event has recovery time | owner-role: on-site lead | playbook: key-person-loss

The 2026 team recorded that one person carrying both the day program and a late event loses the recovery time the next day needs (L-5054).
Judged for a mid-size conference.
```

```{warning} A volunteer drops out without anyone noticing
:class: hb-risk hb-likelihood-medium hb-impact-medium
risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Confirm each next-day shift directly with the person and keep a few unassigned extra helpers | owner-role: volunteer coordinator

A roster that nobody confirmed leaves a room or the desk unstaffed.
```

```{warning} A small mandate has no owner
:class: hb-risk hb-likelihood-medium hb-impact-medium
risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Keep a role map that lists unowned mandates and review it with the whole team at T-2w | owner-role: conference chair

Venue liaison, materials, on-site money and wrap-up are the usual orphans.
```
