# Speakers and speaker care

## Why it matters

Speakers are the program.
Most of them give their time and pay for their own travel, so the conference owes them clear terms, early information and a fair deal on their ticket.
When those terms are vague, speakers fill the gap with assumptions, and the organizers meet them as unbudgeted costs or as disappointment.

A second problem is attrition.
Speakers have withdrawn close to the event at several conferences, which narrows the range of talks and leaves holes in a published schedule.
A third is preparation.
Attendees notice weak or unprepared talks and ask for slides they can follow.

The Plone Foundation has no written rule on speaker compensation as of 2026-11 (S098).
Each team therefore sets its own terms, and the money side is covered in `finance`.
The wider question of what the community expects from speakers and trainers is covered in `community-and-speaker-expectations`.
The steps for choosing talks are in `cfp-and-selection`.
This chapter starts when a proposal is accepted and ends when the speaker has been thanked.
The decisions below come first, and the how-to section holds the working steps.

## Applies when

- `all` Every conference has speakers, so every team makes these decisions.

## Decisions you must make

- {bdg-primary}`DEFAULT` (basis: L-1070, L-3106) **What does a speaker receive, and is the list written down before the call for talks opens?** Past conferences gave speakers free admission, paid some format leaders or housed invited speakers.
  Write the list once, get the treasurer to sign it off and publish it with the call.
  (decide-by: T-8m)
- {bdg-secondary}`OPTION` **Do complimentary speaker tickets go through the same ticketing step as paid tickets?** That step carries the Code of Conduct agreement that `code-of-conduct-operations` requires of ticket buyers, and a ticket issued by hand skips it.
  (decide-by: T-4m)
- {bdg-primary}`DEFAULT` (basis: L-2011, L-3069) **Who owns a speaker withdrawal, and what is the replacement rule?** Late withdrawals recurred at more than one conference.
  Name the owner and the rule before acceptances go out.
  (decide-by: T-4m)
- {bdg-secondary}`OPTION` **Do you pay honoraria for long or special formats?** One conference paid tutorial leaders and panel organizers and kept the payment limited so the call did not feel high-stakes.
  Decide it together with the training terms in `trainings`.
  (decide-by: T-8m)
- {bdg-secondary}`OPTION` **Do you reward early delivery of slides and practice files?** Asking speakers to submit materials in advance mostly failed at one conference, whose organizers suggested a small reward.

## How-to

### Before acceptances go out

1. Write the speaker benefit list: admission, discount, lodging, any honorarium and who pays what.
   Done looks like one page, signed off by the program lead and the treasurer.
1. Add the worst-case cost of the list to the budget, counting every accepted speaker.
   Done looks like a budget line that `finance` can trace to the list.
1. Publish the list with the call for talks.
   Done looks like a public page, linked from the call.
1. Name the owner of speaker withdrawals and write down the replacement rule.
   Done looks like an entry in the Tailoring Record.

### After acceptance

1. Send each speaker a written confirmation with the benefit list, a date for confirming attendance, the Code of Conduct agreement step and a date for slides.
   Done looks like a reply from every speaker or a released slot.
1. Circulate the draft schedule to speakers before it is published.
   Done looks like conflicts raised and settled while the schedule can still change.
1. Collect slides and practice files, then publish them before or at the start of each session.
   Done looks like a link per session on the schedule.
1. Send speakers a short note on staying on topic and on keeping the talk about the subject rather than the presenting organization.
   Done looks like the note sent with the confirmation.

### During and after the event

1. Brief session chairs on the schedule, the time limits and how to handle a no-show.
   Ask chairs to introduce each speaker with some background, not just a name, as a 2026 attendee requested (L-6044).
   Done looks like a chair who knows the replacement rule.
1. If a speaker withdraws, follow the `speaker-dropout` playbook.
   Done looks like the slot filled or released and the schedule updated.
1. Collect feedback on each session and pass it to the speaker.
   A 2026 attendee asked for a feedback form per talk, so that each speaker hears about their own session (L-6045).
   Done looks like a short summary sent within the weeks after the conference.

## Risks

```{warning} Speaker withdraws close to the event
:class: hb-risk hb-likelihood-medium hb-impact-medium
risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Name an owner for withdrawals and agree the replacement rule before acceptances go out | owner-role: program-lead | playbook: speaker-dropout

Withdrawals as late as two weeks before the event narrowed the program at more than one conference.
```

```{warning} Unwritten speaker benefits become unbudgeted cost
:class: hb-risk hb-likelihood-medium hb-impact-high
risk | likelihood: medium | impact: high | mitigation: Publish a signed-off benefit list with the call for talks and cost it at the worst-case speaker count | owner-role: treasurer

Speakers assume terms that the team never wrote down, and first-time organizers meet the bill late.
See `community-and-speaker-expectations` for the wider pattern.
```

```{warning} Slides and practice files arrive late or not at all
:class: hb-risk hb-likelihood-high hb-impact-low
risk | likelihood: high | impact: low | mitigation: Set a delivery date in the speaker confirmation and decide before the call whether to reward early delivery | owner-role: program-lead

Attendees ask for materials before or at the start of a session, and advance requests to speakers mostly failed at one conference.
```
