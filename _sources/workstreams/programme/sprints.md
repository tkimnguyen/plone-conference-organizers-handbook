# Sprints

## Why it matters

The sprint closes the conference week, and it is where the community turns what it heard into work.
Sprints are as much about discussing what was learned, unblocking pull requests, meeting online collaborators and onboarding newcomers as about writing code (L-4234, S084, as of 2026-10).
A team that plans a sprint only as a room with Wi-Fi misses that purpose.

The Foundation requirements register lists post-conference sprints as part of the conference design (as of 2026-11).
Foundation funding for a sprint has its own request deadline and its own report-back.
A late request or a missing report can cost the team the reimbursement.

The main risk is timing: the leaders, the venue and the funding all have to be settled while the conference itself absorbs the team.
This chapter helps you decide the sprint shape early.

## Applies when

- `all` The Foundation expects post-conference sprints at every conference.

## Decisions you must make

- {bdg-danger}`MUST` (req: R-018; as-of: 2026-11-02) **Will the conference include post-conference sprints, and for how many days?** The call for proposals asks for two to three days with an experienced sprint leader, and the day count comes from the call only.
  (decide-by: T-12m)
- {bdg-secondary}`OPTION` **When do you announce the sprint dates and venue?** One conference fixed them early, so the sprint could be announced while people made travel plans (L-1155).
  (decide-by: T-9m)
- {bdg-primary}`DEFAULT` (basis: L-1158, L-4069) **Who leads the sprint, and have they agreed?** Two independent sources advise recruiting experienced community members to lead, and the Foundation's call asks for a leader.
  Recruit the leader or daily coordinators before announcing the sprint.
  (decide-by: T-6m)
- {bdg-secondary}`OPTION` **Where does the sprint run?** Conferences have used the conference hotel, a venue reused from the trainings, a separate inexpensive venue nearby and a café with a coworking space.
  Weigh evening access, Wi-Fi and distance from the hotel.
  (decide-by: T-6m)
- {bdg-secondary}`OPTION` **Is the sprint open without a conference ticket, and are meals provided?** One conference planned an open sprint at a separate venue with no meals, and others provided refreshments and lunch.
  (decide-by: T-4m)
- {bdg-danger}`MUST` (req: R-033; as-of: 2026-11-02) **If you request Foundation sprint funding, is the request filed in time?** File through the plone.org form at least one month ahead for a community sprint, and by December of the prior year for a strategic sprint.
  (decide-by: T-2m)
- {bdg-danger}`MUST` (req: R-032; as-of: 2026-11-02) **Who files the Sponsorship Report Form and the plone.org news item before reimbursement?** A funded event reports back before it is reimbursed.
  Name one person before the sprint starts.

## How-to

### Plan the sprint

1. Fix the sprint days together with the conference days and publish them with the program.
   Done looks like sprint dates on the public schedule.
1. Recruit an experienced community member to lead the sprint, or one coordinator per day.
   Post-conference sprints have drawn 50 to 150 people, and a separate inexpensive venue nearby is fine (L-4069, S049, as of 2015).
   Done looks like a named leader for every sprint day.
1. Book a large room for teams and a smaller quiet room, with power cords, drinks and snacks.
   Done looks like a room plan that the venue has confirmed in writing, including evening access.
1. Decide who gets travel support before you promise any.
   Be strategic and selective when offering to reimburse travel, because the Foundation says reimbursing every participant is not financially possible (L-4049, S005, as of 2026-11).
   Done looks like written criteria and a total that the treasurer has approved.
1. Put sprint organization into the task plan for the month of the conference.
   The Foundation planning schedule places it in September (L-4042, S004, as of 2026-11).
   Done looks like open sprint tasks with owners.
1. Create a separate community wiki or page where attendees list topics and teams before the sprint.
   Done looks like a topic list that exists before the first sprint morning.

### Run the sprint

1. Open with every participant pitching the topic they want to work on.
   Done looks like a board of topics with names against them.
1. Hold short stand-ups, for example at 10:00 and 17:00, and encourage everyone to work with at least one other person.
   Done looks like stand-ups on the schedule.
1. Welcome newcomers explicitly, with a team that helps first-time sprinters, and say what a sprint is for so that non-developers know documentation work belongs there.
   Done looks like a welcome note in the sprint announcement.

### Close the sprint

1. Count the sprinters, in person and online.
   Done looks like numbers a later team can compare.
1. Write the sprint report and the plone.org news item, and file the funding report if the sprint was funded.
   Done looks like a published report and a filed form.

## Risks

```{warning} Sprint leaders are not recruited in time
:class: hb-risk hb-likelihood-medium hb-impact-medium
risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Recruit the leader or daily coordinators before announcing the sprint, and name a deputy for each day | owner-role: sprint-lead

The Foundation's call asks for an experienced leader, and the conference week absorbs the team.
```

```{warning} Funding is lost to a missed deadline or report
:class: hb-risk hb-likelihood-medium hb-impact-medium
risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: File the funding request at least a month ahead and name the person who files the report-back before the sprint starts | owner-role: treasurer

Reimbursement depends on the request and on the report after the event.
```

```{warning} Venue closes before sprinters stop working
:class: hb-risk hb-likelihood-medium hb-impact-low
risk | likelihood: medium | impact: low | mitigation: Agree evening access in the venue booking and confirm it in writing | owner-role: venue-lead

At one conference the hotel agreed a longer evening stay for the people still working.
```

```{warning} Newcomers arrive and find no way in
:class: hb-risk hb-likelihood-medium hb-impact-medium
risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Open with topic pitches, name a team that helps first-time sprinters and explain in the announcement what a sprint is for | owner-role: sprint-lead

Attendees at one conference read sprints as meant only for hard-core programmers and missed a documentation sprint.
```
