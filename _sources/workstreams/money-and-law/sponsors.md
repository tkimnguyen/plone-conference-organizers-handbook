# Sponsors

## Why it matters

Sponsors pay for a large part of a Plone conference, and many of them are companies that already believe in Plone.
They give money and expect to see it used well.
A team that treats sponsorship as a list of logos loses the relationship, and with it the income for the next conference.

As of 2025, the Foundation Board's priorities ask for one straightforward sponsorship program that covers provider, Foundation, conference, individual and GitHub sponsors, managed by a team (L-4061, S092).
Conference sponsorship sits inside that wider picture, so it should not compete with Foundation sponsorship for the same donors without a conversation.

The consequences are plain.
Missing sponsor income shrinks the budget in `finance`.
A sponsor who feels ignored does not return.
The steps are in the How-to section.

## Applies when

- `all` Every Plone conference raises money from sponsors in some form.

## Decisions you must make

- {bdg-primary}`DEFAULT` (basis: L-1040, L-3083) **Who owns sponsor relationships from first contact to the final thanks?** Name one person for sponsors, so that each sponsor feels it got its money's worth. (decide-by: T-12m)
- {bdg-primary}`DEFAULT` (basis: L-1038, L-2006, L-4143) **Will you publish tiered packages with a benefits matrix?** Seattle, Bristol and Tokyo all did. A matrix from a name on the website to a booth and a talk makes the difference between tiers visible, and Seattle's sponsors found the middle and lower tiers unclear. (decide-by: T-12m)
- {bdg-secondary}`OPTION` **Will you open categories beyond companies?** The Foundation Board agreed a category for universities and local governments (L-4120), and Boston listed individual contributors (L-4128). (decide-by: T-9m)
- {bdg-secondary}`OPTION` **Will you accept support from public bodies in services instead of cash?** Brasília expected some government sponsors to pay in services, which lowers real income and offsets expenses and taxes (L-3119), and won a venue discount (L-3093). (decide-by: T-9m)
- {bdg-secondary}`OPTION` **Will you bundle packages with a co-located event?** Brasília bundled packages for two conferences to reach sponsors of the other one (L-3113). (decide-by: T-9m)

## How-to

1. Set the sponsor target from the budget in `finance` and state how far the budget depends on sponsors.
   Done when the budget shows income at a low and an expected level.
1. Name the sponsor owner and a deputy.
   Done when both appear in the team roster.
1. Write the packages: tiers, price in local currency, a benefits matrix and a deadline for each benefit.
   Done when each tier differs from the next in at least one named benefit.
1. Publish the packages on the website and in a prospectus.
   Done when a prospective sponsor can find the packages in two clicks from the home page.
1. Approach existing supporters first, and record every contact and its outcome in a tracker.
   Done when the tracker shows an owner and a next step for every prospect.
1. Think about how to turn groups of attendees from one organization into sponsors as well (L-3081).
   Done when each organization that sends several people has received one offer.
1. Agree every sponsorship in writing, with tier, amount, VAT treatment, due date and benefits. See `contracts-legal-insurance`.
   Done when no benefit is promised that the contract does not name.
1. Invoice, track payments and deliver benefits from a checklist, then report back to each sponsor after the conference.
   Done when every sponsor has received a thank-you message with what was delivered.

## Principles and general lessons

No general lesson has passed the promotion rule yet.
Two statements from single conferences carry weight in the decisions above.
Seattle's sponsor feedback found the top package attractive and the lower tiers unclear, so the organizers concluded the value proposition needed more work (L-1040).
San Francisco found that sponsor handling needed much more attention and recommended one person dedicated to sponsors (L-3083).

## Risks

```{warning} No top-tier sponsor signs
:class: hb-risk hb-likelihood-medium hb-impact-medium
risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Budget on a mix of lower tiers and review the pipeline monthly from T-12m | owner-role: sponsor owner | playbook: cash-flow-crunch

One conference covered the gap with lower-tier sponsors.
Judged for a mid-size conference.
```

```{warning} A sponsor feels it did not get what it paid for
:class: hb-risk hb-likelihood-medium hb-impact-medium
risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Give each sponsor one named contact and deliver benefits from a checklist | owner-role: sponsor owner

Sponsors who feel ignored do not return.
```

```{warning} Sponsor money arrives late
:class: hb-risk hb-likelihood-medium hb-impact-high
risk | likelihood: medium | impact: high | mitigation: Put due dates in the sponsorship agreement and chase overdue invoices at fixed intervals | owner-role: treasurer | playbook: cash-flow-crunch

Early costs may fall due before the first sponsor payment.
```

```{warning} In-kind support is counted as cash
:class: hb-risk hb-likelihood-low hb-impact-medium
risk | likelihood: low | impact: medium | mitigation: Record in-kind support in a separate budget line and agree its value in writing | owner-role: treasurer

Support in services lowers cash income, even when it offsets expenses.
```

```{warning} Attendee data is shared with sponsors without permission
:class: hb-risk hb-likelihood-low hb-impact-high
risk | likelihood: low | impact: high | mitigation: Share attendee contact data only with documented opt-in consent, as set out in `privacy-gdpr` | owner-role: sponsor owner

Sponsors ask for contact lists as a benefit.
```
