# Playbook: low ticket sales

Open this playbook when ticket sales fall behind the plan at a checkpoint.
It covers the first hour of the response; pricing and the sales plan are in `ticketing-and-registration` and `marketing-and-outreach`.

## Triggers

- Sales at a checkpoint are below the dated forecast after allowing for the community's late-purchase pattern.
  Organizer anxiety alone is not a trigger; a gap against the forecast is (L-5009).
- Sales are below the break-even count with four weeks or less to go.
- Catering, shirt or room-block orders are due and the team cannot fix the numbers.
- Funding or support tied to an attendance threshold, such as a convention-bureau offer, is at risk.
- One ticket category, such as training or the business track, sells far below its own plan while the rest is on track.

## First-hour actions

1. Pull the dated sales by ticket category and compare them with the forecast and, where available, with the sales curves of earlier editions.
   Done looks like one table with sold, forecast and break-even for each category.
2. Decide whether the gap is timing or demand.
   If sales follow the usual late curve, record the decision and the date of the next check, and stop here.
3. Ask registered attendees to say publicly that they are coming, because visible attendance encourages last-minute registrations (L-4133).
4. Ask people who already know they are coming to buy their tickets, and say why the numbers matter: food, shirts and rooms.
5. Test the purchase path as a buyer, including buying several tickets in one order and paying by invoice.
   Remove any friction found the same day.
6. List the reversible costs and the last date each can still be reduced: catering counts, shirts, print runs, room blocks.
   Do not cut any of them before that date.
7. If commitments already exceed confirmed income, open [Playbook: cash-flow crunch](cash-flow-crunch.md).
8. Report to the team: the gap, the timing-or-demand judgment, the actions, and the next checkpoint.

### What history says

- In 2012 sales stood below the break-even count four weeks before the event, and a late rush carried the total well past it (L-2084 in `ticketing-and-registration#case-notes`).
  The Conference Committee's own advice is that people buy tickets very late, and that sales should stay open until very late (L-4044, L-4045).
- In 2018 the organizers publicly asked everyone who knew they were coming to buy tickets without delay, because they needed the numbers for shirts and food (L-4146).
  Buying several tickets in one order arrived only late in that sales period (L-4147).
- In 2016 attendees were asked, days before the start, to share their attendance on social media to encourage last-minute registrations (L-4133).
- In 2010 feedback showed that registrations depend on announcing talks and speakers early, and in 2006 confirmed headline sessions supported a sold-out conference (L-2012, L-1063).
- Bids have planned for more participants than came: up to 700 against just over 400, and about 200 against around 150 (L-4089, L-4142).
- In 2026 the organizers produced attendance-prediction research for decisions during late sales; a reusable, sanitized version is planned (L-5030, L-5029, L-5066).
