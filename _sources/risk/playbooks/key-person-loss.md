# Playbook: key-person loss

Open this playbook when a person who holds an essential role cannot continue, for a day or for good.
It covers the first hour; roles, deputies and the team structure are in `team-and-governance` and `volunteers-and-roles`.

## Triggers

- An organizer with an exclusive role falls ill, has a family emergency, or loses the time to continue.
- A person shows signs of exhaustion, often because they hold daytime operations and a late social event at once.
- The only holder of an account, a bank access, a domain or a vendor relationship cannot be reached.
- A person leaves the team in the middle of planning.
- Knowledge of the agenda, the budget or a contract sits in one person's head or private files.

## First-hour actions

1. Find out how long the person will be unavailable, and share with the team only what the person agrees to share.
   The team needs availability, not reasons.
2. Open the role map and the role's narrative, and name the deputy who takes over.
   If no deputy exists, the conference chair takes the role for the first day and names one.
3. Give the deputy the role's open tasks, deadlines and pending decisions, in writing.
4. Check that the deputy can reach every account, inbox and payment tool the role uses, through the shared password manager or the Foundation.
   Done looks like the deputy logging in to each one.
5. Tell the vendors, speakers or sponsors in contact with the role who their contact is during the absence.
6. Postpone or cancel any decision only the absent person could make, and record the replacement date.
7. If the deputy also holds a role, take something off their list at the same time, so the handover does not create a second loss.
8. Drop optional work that has no owner, and record the drop in the Tailoring Record.

## Owner roles

| Role | Responsibility | Full advice in |
|---|---|---|
| Conference chair | Names the deputy, rebalances workloads and approves drops. | `team-and-governance` |
| Deputy of the role | Takes over the open tasks, accesses and contacts. | `volunteers-and-roles` |
| Volunteer coordinator | Moves helpers to cover on-site gaps and confirms their shifts. | `volunteers-and-roles` |
| Treasurer | Restores access to bank and payment accounts and checks pending payments. | `finance` |
| Conference Committee contact | Brings in experience from past teams when no one on the team knows the role. | `handover-contract` |

### What history says

- In 2011 the person responsible for the Wi-Fi was hospitalized two weeks before the conference, and there was no backup plan (L-3075 in `av-recording-internet#case-notes`).
- In 2006 having a second person who knew every session and presenter was extremely helpful for building the agenda (L-1077 in `scheduling#case-notes`).
- In 2010 a colleague inside the host company supported the Wi-Fi specialist, so the network did not rest on one person (L-2047).
- In 2010 the working budget lived in a spreadsheet whose history successors could not see (L-2025).
- In 2026 the organizers found that one person owning daytime operations and a late social event lost all recovery time, and advised delegating a complete responsibility to a deputy (L-5054 in `volunteers-and-roles#risks`).
  They also advised separating the responsible manager's coordination from routine helper work, and confirming each assignment directly before the next shift (L-5051, L-5053).
- The Conference Committee exists to keep knowledge of processes, people and vendors between editions (L-4023).
