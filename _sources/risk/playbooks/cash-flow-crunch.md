# Playbook: cash-flow crunch

Open this playbook when the conference cannot pay a committed bill on its due date from money it already holds.
It covers the first hour; the budget and cash-flow plan are in `finance`.

## Triggers

- The dated cash view shows a committed payment falling due before enough confirmed income arrives to cover it.
- A sponsor payment, grant or Foundation support that the budget counted has not arrived and has no confirmed date.
- An organizer is fronting conference costs privately beyond what the team agreed, or a personal card limit is close.
- The final-week bills for catering, equipment and network rental arrive together and exceed the balance.
- A vendor has not sent an invoice, so a payment or a reimbursement cannot be processed.
- An expectation nobody budgeted, such as trainer accommodation, turns into a bill.

## First-hour actions

1. Freeze further commitments: the treasurer tells the team that no additional spending is agreed without sign-off until the review is done.
2. Build a dated cash view on one page: every committed payment by due date against confirmed income only.
   Leave out unanswered grant requests, unpaid sponsor pledges and forecast ticket sales.
3. Separate a timing gap from a shortfall.
   A timing gap closes when expected income arrives; a shortfall does not.
   If the doubt is about ticket sales, open [Playbook: low ticket sales](low-ticket-sales.md) in parallel.
4. Invoice every sponsor who has agreed but not yet been invoiced, the same day.
5. Call the two or three largest creditors and ask to move or split the next due date, before it is missed.
6. If the Foundation acts as financial agent, inform the Foundation treasurer with the dated cash view.
   If not, ask the Foundation in writing whether it offers an advance under its current terms.
7. Stop any private fronting beyond the agreed limit.
   Record every private advance as a loan with an amount, a holder and a repayment date in the team's private books.
8. Report to the whole team: the gap, its date, the actions taken, and the time of the next review.

## Owner roles

| Role | Responsibility | Full advice in |
|---|---|---|
| Treasurer | Builds the cash view, freezes spending, and talks to creditors. | `finance` |
| Conference chair | Decides on scope cuts and on any private advance, and informs the Foundation. | `team-and-governance` |
| Foundation liaison | Asks for an advance or for the financial agent's decision, in writing. | `legal-entity-and-financial-agent` |
| Sponsor lead | Turns agreed sponsorships into invoices and chases payment dates. | `sponsors` |
| Ticketing lead | Supplies the dated sales figures and the payout schedule of the ticket platform. | `ticketing-and-registration` |

### What history says

- In 2006 the host could not advance the costs due before income arrived, and the Foundation lent it a seed loan, repaid after the conference; without it the organizers would have declined to host (L-1031 in `finance#case-notes`).
  The up-front costs in that budget were far larger than the loan (L-1032).
- In 2010 the budget was built on three attendance levels, with break-even below the hoped-for number; the final account covered every external cost but not the host's staff time (L-2020, L-2029).
- In 2011 the lead organizer fronted costs for later reimbursement and carried a large personal card balance until the card issuer raised the limit (L-3008).
  The same team found that the largest bills arrive at the end, and that a missing venue invoice blocked reimbursement a month after the event (L-3007, L-3010).
- In 2013 the host association had no cash at the start of the year, and loans from friends paid the first publicity and emergency costs until sponsorship money arrived (L-3096).
  A cash-flow spreadsheet checked often against the bank and payment providers kept the year under control (L-3097).
- The 2016 call for proposals told bidders that they would almost certainly need to put down money before sponsorship and registration income came in (L-4064).
  The requirement to explain cash-flow management is unverifiable as a standing rule as of 2026-11-02 (R-006), and whether the Foundation still offers advances is an open question (R-037).
- In 2026 the organizers advised planning cash flow for a community that buys late, and keeping unconfirmed grant applications out of committed income (L-5008, L-5006).
