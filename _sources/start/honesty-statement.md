# Honesty statement

A handbook that hides its weak spots is worse than no handbook.
This page states the limits of the Plone Conference Organizers Handbook in one place, so that you can decide how far to trust it.
The short version is in [About and purpose](about.md).
The figures below are as of 2026-11-02.

## It is advice, not policy

The handbook is advice from organizers to organizers.
It is not Plone Foundation policy, it was not approved by the Foundation board or the Conference Committee, and it is not legal, tax or insurance advice.
Where the Foundation or the law requires something, the item carries the MUST badge and cites a row of the [Foundation requirements register](../evidence/requirements-register.md) with an as-of date.
A MUST row that the handbook could not verify against a live Foundation text is not allowed to carry a MUST, and the chapter says so.
Everything else is a DEFAULT or an OPTION, and you may deviate by recording your reason; see [Tailor](tailor.md).

Jurisdiction annexes are general information.
Check them with a qualified local adviser before you rely on them.

## Whose experience this is

The evidence is not a random sample of Plone conferences.
Three biases are known.

**Funded companies dominate.**
Most conferences in the archive were run by a company or an established group that could afford to write things down, and most sources are their blog posts, recaps and call responses.
Conferences with a tiny volunteer budget, cancelled conferences and conferences that never started left almost no record.
The advice therefore leans toward medium-sized, adequately funded, in-person events.

**Survivorship.**
Only conferences that happened can teach us what they did.
A practice that sank an organizing team is under-represented, because the team did not write a recap.
Read an absence of warnings as weak evidence of safety.

**One year is over-represented.**
Of 776 ledger rows, 165 come from Seattle 2006 and 132 from Maastricht 2026, with a further 35 Foundation-level rows dated 2026.
Several editions are represented by five rows or fewer.
The 2026 team wrote the handbook, so its own year is described in the most detail, and no second conference has used it yet.
No page is at maturity `proven`.
The 2026 attendee survey drew 21 responses over about three weeks (L-6002), and the raw responses are held back for re-identification reasons.

Most evidence comes from Europe and North America.
The 2020 and 2021 online editions are thinly documented.
Specific lessons show their year, place and age, so you can discount them yourself.
A lesson from 2006 can still be right, and the label `stale-prone` asks you to check the source before you rely on it.

Specific lessons from 2011 cite either "Plone Conference 2011 organizing team" or "Plone Foundation" as their organization, depending on which group extracted them, and rows from 2026 cite "Plone Conference 2026 organizing team".
If you merge lessons by organization, treat these names as the same bodies.

## What we do not know

Ten areas were declared thin before writing began.

- Hybrid and online formats.
- Accessibility.
- Legal, tax and visa questions outside the EU and the United States.
- Sponsorship in a weak market.
- How ticket demand responds to price.
- Small-budget and volunteer-run conferences.
- Health, safety and safeguarding.
- Sustainability.
- Jurisdiction annexes, which all start as stubs.
- The 2026 post-mortem, which is a marked draft.

The [coverage map](../generated/coverage-map.md) shows the maturity of every page and the number of thin markers in each of the ten areas, including the areas that have none.
The [thin registry](../generated/thin-registry.md) lists every marker with what would close the gap and who might know.
Together they are the honest answer to the question what the handbook does not yet cover.

Beyond the ten areas, the requirements register holds twelve rows that exist only in older calls for proposals, and four that are written as open questions to the Foundation.
They are marked as unverifiable or pending, and they cannot back a MUST until the Foundation answers.

The handbook has also not had an independent review of its whole text.
A drafting team worked with agent assistance, every claim cites a ledger row, and the review gate is still ahead as of 2026-11-02.
Treat all pages as drafts until a page says otherwise in its front matter.

## Privacy and what is left out

Only generalized information, rules, guidelines, risks and pointers enter the handbook.
That is the sanitization rule of the project, and a script checks it on every change.
The handbook holds no personal data, no private amounts, no contact details of private persons and no ticket-buyer data.
Lessons name organizations and places, never private persons.
Prices, tax rates and fees appear only as dated, sourced examples.

Some sources are held back from the public bibliography because their content could identify a respondent or a private party.
Raw survey submissions are not published.
Conference budgets from the organizers' private books are generalized or left out.
The cost of this rule is a loss of concrete detail, and the handbook accepts that cost.
If a page feels vague where you expected a number, check whether the number was private.

The archive of source copies that backs the bibliography keeps each source's own license and does not relicense third-party material.
Republishing any of it is a separate decision that needs a licensing and privacy check first.

## How you can hold us to this page

- Open an issue if a page claims more than its evidence supports, or if a gap is not marked.
- Close a gap with a lesson or a chapter; see [Update](../running/update.md) and [Extend](../running/extend.md).
- Tell us when a number is wrong.
  Every figure carries an as-of date and a source ID, so a correction is a small change.

The aim is a handbook whose limits are as visible as its advice.
