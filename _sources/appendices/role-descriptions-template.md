# Template: role descriptions

This page holds ten role cards you can copy into your own planning repository.
A role card is a one-page description of one job at one conference.
It tells a person what they own, what they hand to whom, and what a normal day looks like.
The cards are starting points.
Change the names, the days and the boundaries until they match your team.

Two chapters carry the reasoning behind the cards.
[Volunteers and roles](../workstreams/people/volunteers-and-roles.md) explains how to staff and treat helpers.
[Team and governance](../job/team-and-governance.md) explains how to split the work between a lead and the owners of each area.

## The card pattern

Every card has the same fields, so a reader can compare two cards side by side and find the handoff between them.

| Field | What to write |
|---|---|
| Role | The job title. Use the title the incident playbooks use, so a playbook and a card name the same person. |
| Holder | The person who holds the role. Leave the placeholder in the template. |
| Deputy | The person who can take over the open tasks, accesses and contacts. A card without a deputy is not finished. |
| Reports to | The one role that hears about problems first. |
| Purpose | One sentence on what the role is for. |
| Owns | Three to five outcomes the role is responsible for. |
| Does not own | The neighboring jobs people often assume belong here. |
| Hands off to, receives from | The roles on each side of the handoffs. |
| Decides alone, asks the chair | The edge of the role's authority. |
| Day in the life | Planning phase, final weeks, on site, afterward. |
| Access needed | The accounts and keys the deputy must also hold. |
| Done when | The observable end state. |

The pattern combines three sources.
The day-in-the-life storyline comes from the 2026 on-site roles model, which wrote one story per role and read the stories against each other to find tasks nobody owned (S101, L-5050).
The split between the responsible manager and the routine helpers comes from the same model (L-5051).
The one-owner-per-area idea comes from the 2012 Arnhem team, which published its role split so attendees and sponsors knew whom to contact (S037, L-2111 to L-2116).

## How to fill in the cards

1. Copy the cards for the roles you need into a single file in your planning repository. Done when the file lists every role and every name.
1. Replace each placeholder holder and deputy with a person who has said yes. A role that has no holder yet keeps the placeholder, and the chair lists it as open.
1. Rewrite "Day in the life" in the voice of the person who holds the role, from the moment they wake up to the moment they hand over. Done when the holder has read it and corrected it.
1. Read each card together with the cards on both sides of its handoffs. Most failures on site happen at a handoff, not inside a role (S101, L-5050).
1. Add the smaller mandates that no card covers, such as materials, on-site money, newcomer welcome and post-event wrap-up, and give each a name (L-5055).
1. Confirm every helper assignment with the person directly before the shift starts. A published roster alone does not show that the person accepted the handoff (L-5053).
1. Check the combined workload of anyone who owns daytime operations and a late social event. Give a deputy one complete responsibility if the recovery time would disappear (L-5054).

## The cards

### Conference chair

````text
Role: Conference chair
Holder: <name>
Deputy: <name>
Reports to: The organizing entity and the Plone Foundation contact
Purpose: Keep the whole conference pointed at one set of goals and one calendar.
Owns:
- the written goals and the scope of the event
- the master timeline and the freeze dates
- decisions that cut across workstreams, including cuts to reversible costs
- every public statement in a crisis
Does not own: the daily running of any workstream. Each has an owner.
Hands off to: owners of each area. Receives from: treasurer, program lead, on-site lead.
Decides alone: scope cuts at their last date, who the deputy of a lost role is.
Asks the Foundation: anything that touches the name, the money or the financial agent.
Day in the life
  Planning: runs the team meeting, reads the open-risk list, clears blockers.
  Final weeks: holds the freeze reviews and the readiness meeting.
  On site: stays free of tasks so attention can move to the gap that appears.
  Afterward: signs off the handover and the post-mortem.
Access needed: bank oversight, domain and website admin, contract copies.
Done when: the handover is filed and the next team has the dated timeline.
````

### Treasurer

````text
Role: Treasurer
Holder: <name>
Deputy: <name>
Reports to: Conference chair
Purpose: Keep the conference able to pay what it has promised.
Owns:
- the budget at three attendance levels and the dated cash view
- invoices, payments, refunds and the books
- the sales forecast and the break-even count
- the paper trail for the financial agent or accountant
Does not own: ticket prices (ticketing lead) or sponsor negotiation (sponsor lead).
Hands off to: ticketing lead, sponsor lead. Receives from: both, plus venue and suppliers.
Decides alone: payment timing inside the agreed budget.
Asks the chair: any commitment above the agreed limit.
Day in the life
  Planning: sets up budget and accounting when ticket sales begin.
  Final weeks: weekly import of sales, comparison with the forecast.
  On site: holds the float, signs for cash costs.
  Afterward: closes the books and files the budget model.
Access needed: bank view, ticket platform reports, payment provider dashboard.
Done when: budget and actuals agree and the books are closed.
````

### Program lead

````text
Role: Program lead
Holder: <name>
Deputy: <name>
Reports to: Conference chair
Purpose: Build a program the community wants and that runs on time.
Owns:
- the call for talks, the selection and the acceptances
- the schedule grid and every change to it
- keynotes and invited speakers, with the speaker care lead
- the program freeze
Does not own: room setup (venue liaison) or recording (AV and recording lead).
Hands off to: speaker care lead, communications lead. Receives from: review panel, sponsors with talk slots.
Decides alone: the replacement when a speaker drops out.
Asks the chair: any change after the program freeze.
Day in the life
  Planning: opens the call, chases proposals, runs the review.
  Final weeks: publishes the schedule, briefs session chairs.
  On site: watches the clock, handles swaps and no-shows.
  Afterward: collects recordings and speaker feedback.
Access needed: submission system, schedule tool, speaker contact list.
Done when: every slot has a confirmed speaker and the schedule is published.
````

### Venue liaison

````text
Role: Venue liaison
Holder: <name>
Deputy: <name>
Reports to: On-site lead
Purpose: Be the one person the venue talks to, before and during the event.
Owns:
- the venue contract terms as they affect the team day to day
- room setup: projectors, furniture, power, signs
- catering orders and changes together with the venue
- the list of venue contacts for every shift
Does not own: network design (AV and recording lead) or contract signature (chair or treasurer).
Hands off to: on-site lead, AV and recording lead. Receives from: venue staff.
Decides alone: small setup changes inside the contract.
Asks the on-site lead: anything that costs money or moves a session.
Day in the life
  Planning: walks the rooms, gets the emergency procedure in writing.
  Final weeks: confirms headcount for catering and the room plan.
  On site: first in the building, walks each room before doors open.
  Afterward: settles the final bill and the damage check.
Access needed: venue contact numbers, room keys or badges.
Done when: every room is ready before each day and the venue has signed off.
````

### On-site lead

````text
Role: On-site lead
Holder: <name>
Deputy: <name>
Reports to: Conference chair
Purpose: Keep the days running and be the one who escalates to the chair.
Owns:
- the run of show for each day
- the daily operations meeting and the end-of-day readiness check
- the registration desk and the information desk
- the emergency card with venue, party and medical contacts
Does not own: the content of sessions or the money.
Hands off to: venue liaison, volunteer coordinator, AV and recording lead. Receives from: all of them.
Decides alone: schedule moves of a few minutes, who covers a gap.
Asks the chair: cancelling a session or changing the party plan.
Day in the life
  Planning: writes the run of show and the load-in list.
  Final weeks: runs the rehearsal after the last freeze.
  On site: roves, keeps hands empty so attention can move.
  Afterward: writes the day-by-day log for the post-mortem.
Access needed: radios, master attendee list, emergency card.
Done when: the last room is cleared and the log is written.
````

### Volunteer coordinator

````text
Role: Volunteer coordinator
Holder: <name>
Deputy: <name>
Reports to: On-site lead
Purpose: Turn willing attendees into a rostered, briefed and thanked crew.
Owns:
- the helper call, the pool and the roster
- shift rotation, so helpers can attend sessions
- the volunteer briefing, including where to send a report
- meals, the volunteer room and the thank-you
Does not own: the work of each room, which the room owner assigns.
Hands off to: every role that has helpers. Receives from: helpers and role owners.
Decides alone: who covers a shift.
Asks the on-site lead: any change to the number of helpers per slot.
Day in the life
  Planning: sends the call, builds the pool, drafts the roster.
  Final weeks: confirms each helper by name the evening before every shift.
  On site: runs the volunteer room and swaps shifts.
  Afterward: compares the thank-you list with the roster.
Access needed: helper contact list, roster tool.
Done when: every slot on every day has a confirmed name.
````

### Sponsor lead

````text
Role: Sponsor lead
Holder: <name>
Deputy: <name>
Reports to: Conference chair
Purpose: Own every sponsor relationship from first contact to the final thanks.
Owns:
- the sponsor packages and the benefits matrix
- contact with sponsors and the status of each
- the delivery list of promised benefits
- the sponsor section of the website and the thank-you
Does not own: invoicing and payment (treasurer).
Hands off to: treasurer for invoices, communications lead for logos. Receives from: sponsors.
Decides alone: how a benefit is delivered.
Asks the chair: any new benefit, any discount, any sponsor outside the usual categories.
Day in the life
  Planning: publishes packages and reaches out.
  Final weeks: collects logos and speaker names, checks entitlements.
  On site: greets sponsors, resolves booth and ticket questions.
  Afterward: sends thanks and the impact summary.
Access needed: sponsor contact list, benefits checklist.
Done when: every promised benefit is ticked and every sponsor is thanked.
````

### Communications and marketing lead

````text
Role: Communications and marketing lead
Holder: <name>
Deputy: <name>
Reports to: Conference chair
Purpose: Tell the right people the right facts at the right time.
Owns:
- the marketing plan, with one channel per audience
- the website, email and social channels
- the website quality gate before the event
- the holding statement and updates during an incident
Does not own: the program content or the sponsor relationship.
Hands off to: program lead for speaker news, sponsor lead for logos. Receives from: all workstreams.
Decides alone: timing and wording of routine posts.
Asks the chair: any statement about a crisis, the name or the money.
Day in the life
  Planning: sets up accounts and the first announcements.
  Final weeks: runs the quality gate, sends the logistics mail.
  On site: posts updates and keeps remote followers informed.
  Afterward: publishes thanks, recordings and the survey.
Access needed: website admin, email list, social accounts.
Done when: every channel carries the same schedule and the recap is out.
````

### AV and recording lead

````text
Role: AV and recording lead
Holder: <name>
Deputy: <name>
Reports to: On-site lead
Purpose: Make every session audible, visible and recorded.
Owns:
- projectors, microphones, mixer and capture hardware
- the recording workflow, from file destination to upload
- the network plan with the venue
- the daily check that recordings reached their owner
Does not own: room furniture (venue liaison) or the schedule (program lead).
Hands off to: communications lead for published video. Receives from: venue technicians.
Decides alone: equipment swaps and the backup recorder.
Asks the on-site lead: pausing a stream or moving a session.
Day in the life
  Planning: lists the equipment and books what is missing.
  Final weeks: rehearses recording with the real hardware through playback.
  On site: tests the network under load on setup day, checks files each evening.
  Afterward: converts and publishes within the agreed days.
Access needed: recorder drives, video host account.
Done when: the playlist is public and linked from the program page.
````

### Code of Conduct response lead

````text
Role: Code of Conduct response lead
Holder: <name>
Deputy: <name>
Reports to: Conference chair, for support, and acts independently of the chair on cases
Purpose: Receive reports, decide interim measures and own the follow-up.
Owns:
- the reporting channels and the response team
- the written record of every report
- interim measures during the event
- the briefing for volunteers on where to send a report
Does not own: emergencies, which go to the safety contact first.
Hands off to: safety contact for risks to safety. Receives from: any participant or volunteer.
Decides alone: interim measures needed to keep people safe.
Asks the chair: public statements.
Day in the life
  Planning: names the team, writes the briefing, rehearses the first hour.
  Final weeks: confirms two people answer the contact route.
  On site: stays reachable all day, with a second responder at every conversation.
  Afterward: closes cases and files an anonymized summary.
Access needed: a private record store only the response team can read.
Done when: every report has a recorded outcome and a follow-up.
````

## Playbook roles without a card

The incident playbooks name more roles than the ten cards.
Each of them fits into a card, so the playbooks and the cards never name different people for the same duty.

| Playbook role | Where it fits |
|---|---|
| Ticketing lead, registration lead | Treasurer for money and reports, on-site lead for the desk. Give the ticketing lead its own card if one person sells tickets for a long period. |
| Speaker care lead, training coordinator | Program lead. Split off when the program has many speakers or trainings. |
| Network owner, recording lead | AV and recording lead. |
| Communications lead, marketing lead | The communications and marketing lead card. |
| Safety contact | On-site lead, named on the emergency card. A separate person is better on a large event. |
| Foundation liaison | Conference chair, unless the team names a second person for the financial agent. |
| Online lead | Needed only for hybrid or online formats. Write a card from the AV and recording lead card. |
| Deputy of the role | Every card has a deputy field. |

## The 2012 split, mapped to the cards

The Arnhem team published six functional roles.
This table shows where each lands in the ten cards.
It is a mapping for orientation, not advice to copy the 2012 split.

| 2012 role | What it owned | Closest card |
|---|---|---|
| Attendee manager | The bid, registration, the budget, the information desk, and questions about accommodation, fees, money and sponsoring (L-2111). | Treasurer and on-site lead |
| Social activity manager | City information and the companion program (L-2112). | On-site lead, or a separate social owner |
| Venue and sprint manager | Venue contact, projectors, furniture, coffee and lunch, lightning talks and sprints (L-2113). | Venue liaison |
| Program manager | The day-by-day program and care of the keynote and invited speakers (L-2114). | Program lead |
| Marketing manager | Press, website, blog and social media before the event, reports during it (L-2115). | Communications and marketing lead |
| Party manager | The conference party, including music, equipment and acts (L-2116). | A separate owner, or the on-site lead on a small event |

## Applies when

Use the cards for any conference with a team of more than three people.
A very small team can merge several cards into one person, as long as each card still has a deputy.
