Reading the evidence#

These are specific, dated observations, not general recommendations. General lessons require three independent conferences across two organizing bodies, or a structural reason independent of year. See How to read lessons for promotion and ranking. The pinned reference year is 2026; ages 0–3 are recent, 4–8 are mid, and older evidence is stale-prone. General lessons rank first; specific lessons sort by year descending, confidence (observed, reported, hearsay), then first ledger ID. Conflicting counts and populations remain separate; stream viewers, sprinters and registrants are not interchangeable. A dash means no matching ledger evidence; it does not mean zero attendance.

Evidence notes#

All placed rows are preserved below, including contextual observations without an attendance count.

[1] (1,2,3,4)

L-1001, as of 2006, S009, observed, active. Plone Conference 2006 ran three conference days in late October 2006 with about 350 attendees from 35 U.S. states and 20 countries, preceded by two days of training for over 110 students and followed by a two-day sprint with about 50 participants.

[2] (1,2,3)

L-1002, as of 2006, S086, observed, active. The 2006 evaluation (about 140 respondents) profiled the audience: business 41%, technology consulting 34%, nonprofit 31%, education 26%, government 16% and scientific research 11% (multiple answers allowed); about 22% had used Plone little or not at all before attending.

[3] (1,2,3,4)

L-4001, as of 2006, S002, reported, active. The Foundation's conference history page reports 350 attendees for the 2006 Seattle conference.

[10] (1,2,3)

L-3003, as of 2011, S023, observed, active. Per the 2011 organizers' wiki (as of 2011): about USD 18K in sponsorship and USD 79K in ticket income against about USD 95K in costs, leaving roughly USD 8K for the Plone Foundation plus USD 690 raised for next year's scholarships.

[11] (1,2,3)

L-3013, as of 2011, S024, observed, active. Per the 2011 wiki (as of 2011): 16 free tickets (8 to volunteers), 103 early-bird, 30 speaker, 7 student, 104 full-price and 5 other tickets, for USD 76,050 in ticket income. The Budget page rounds ticket income to USD 79K, so the two pages disagree slightly.

[14] (1,2,3)

L-3098, as of 2013, S093, observed, active. Per the published 2013 record (as of 2013): about BRL 215K income, of which about BRL 165K came from sponsorship and only about BRL 24K from conference and training registration; the surplus of about USD 10.4K was split, USD 5K to the Plone Foundation and the rest to the Brazilian Python association.

[15] (1,2,3,4)

L-3104, as of 2013, S093, observed, active. Published 2013 figures: 584 registrants, more than 144 talks, more than 30 international speakers, 7 keynotes, 16 trainings with about 150 trainees, 21 Brazilian states, 19 countries, about 10 percent women, 15 sponsors and 18 supporters.
