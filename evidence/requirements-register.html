Foundation requirements register#

What the register is#

The Plone Foundation has never published one list of what a conference must do. Its own board named a written list of conference and sprint requirements as a 2025 priority, which confirms that none existed (L-4060). In 2023 an organizer had already asked for written guidelines (L-4190). The requirements existed anyway, scattered across Foundation resolutions, calls for proposals, a planning schedule, sponsorship policy pages and committee minutes. Teams met them as expectations that nobody had written down in one place.

This register is the handbook's attempt at that list. It is Level A of a two-level model. Level A is what a document says: a requirement located in a Foundation text, with its source and the date a person last checked it. Level B is what the Foundation itself confirms in answer to a question, and the ask is described below. Nothing here is Foundation policy. The handbook is advisory, and the register records what the sources say, not what the Foundation has agreed to.

Read this register as a basis for negotiations, not as a rulebook. It is the draft of a letter of intent between the Foundation and an organising team: what the Plone Conference should be, and how it is handled. Which rows bind a given edition depends on the organisational model the Board and the team choose, and every edition should fix that choice in writing at bid time.

Two organisational models#

The rows that pass risk and money between the Board and the organiser read differently under each model.

Model 1: the Foundation carries the risk. The Foundation, acting as financial agent, assumes the financial risk and keeps any surplus (R-008, R-009). In exchange the Board can dictate rules about budget and planning: regular updates, a Board member in the planning, final authority on financial decisions, and the share of registration revenue (R-010). The rows marked conditional in this register bind under this model.

Model 2: the organiser carries the risk. The organising company or group bears the full risk and keeps any surplus. This was the de-facto model before the 2017 financial policy and remains the common one (L-4056). The Foundation's involvement is the naming approval, the community floor and the conference timeline, and the budget and planning rows become guidance the organiser adopts or negotiates.

Hybrids exist. Brasília 2013 replaced the percentage share with a fixed fee (L-3116), and Eibar 2023 ended in a donation (L-4189). Some rows bind under every model, because they are the community floor rather than conference rules: the naming approval (R-001, R-002) as a trademark matter, insurance due diligence (R-012) as legal prudence, the Code of Conduct agreement (R-013) as community policy, and the photo consent (R-014) as privacy practice. Everything else is the negotiation surface, and the outcome belongs in a short letter of intent per edition.

The register backs the MUST badge. A decision with the MUST badge cites one row by ID and carries that row's as-of date, for example req: R-012; as-of: 2026-11-02. When a row is re-verified, every badge that cites it fails the build until the date is updated, which is the intended behavior.

As of 2026-11-02#

The register was last verified as of 2026-11-02 and has 37 rows, R-001 to R-037. Twenty rows are verified-current, one is verified-changed, twelve are unverifiable and four are unverified-pending-Foundation-ask. The four pending rows (R-034 to R-037) are written as questions, because no Foundation text read states an answer: the length and streaming of the slot for the Foundation's annual member meeting, the VAT position of a university organizer compared with a company, whether the Foundation sells conference tickets itself, and whether it still offers seed loans, advances or the financial-agent model.

Ten questions to the Foundation would turn the pending and unverifiable rows into confirmed ones. They are collected as one ask bundle and have not been sent as of 2026-11-02. Sending them is a human decision that is still open as of 2026-11-02. Until an answer arrives, treat every row marked unverifiable or unverified-pending-Foundation-ask as a question to put to the Conference Committee at the start of your planning, not as a settled rule.

How the YAML relates to this page#

The file data/requirements-register.yml is the source of truth. The lint rule N02 reads it, not this page. This page mirrors it as of 2026-11-02, in a shorter form: it leaves out the status note, the verification date per row and the source locators, to keep the table readable. For those columns, and for the source IDs behind each row, see data/requirements-register.yml. Two public role addresses in the YAML text are replaced here by a description, so that no address appears on a published page.

If the two disagree, the YAML wins, and the page needs a correction through Update.

Read the badge column with the status column. A badge such as "MUST pending confirmation" belongs to a row that cannot yet back a MUST. Conditional badges such as "MUST (when funded)" apply only to a team that takes the Foundation's money or accounts.

Notes on single rows#

Row R-010 records the Foundation's share of per-person registration revenue, which moved from 15 percent to 20 percent in 2018 and back to 15 percent in 2019 and 2020. A 2013 bid shows that the share is negotiable in kind: because registration was planned to be free, the bid offered the Foundation a fixed payment instead of the usual 15 percent share (L-3114). Read the rate as a figure to confirm with the Foundation, not as a constant.

Using the register#

  1. Start from the chapter you are working on and find its rows in the last column.

  2. Check the status before you rely on a row.

  3. For a row that cannot back a MUST, ask the Conference Committee, and record the answer in your Tailoring Record (see Tailor).

  4. Deviate from a MUST only with a written Foundation exception, linked in the record.

  5. Send back what you learn, so that the next verification starts from your answer: Update.
