Playbook: Code of Conduct incident#

Open this playbook when someone reports behavior that may break the Plone Code of Conduct, or when a team member sees it happen. It covers the first hour; the response team, its training and its procedures are in code-of-conduct-operations.

Triggers#

  • A person reports an incident to a team member, a volunteer, the information desk or the published response contact.

  • A team member or volunteer observes behavior that may break the Code of Conduct.

  • A report arrives about conduct in a conference channel, at a social event or at the sprint venue.

  • A report includes a threat to someone's safety; the safety response comes first.

First-hour actions#

  1. Check that everyone is safe. If anyone is in danger, call the local emergency number first; it is in the volunteer briefing.

  2. Hand the report to the Code of Conduct response lead, or the deputy, without discussing it with anyone else.

  3. Take the report in a private place with two responders present. Listen, write down what was said, the time and the place, and make no promise about the outcome.

  4. Ask the reporter what they need in the moment, for example company, a quiet room, or not to meet the other person.

  5. If needed, separate the people involved at once, and take any interim measure the response team is authorized to take.

  6. Keep the report inside the response team. Store notes in the team's confidential log, never in the public tracker, the repository or a shared channel.

  7. Agree with the reporter when and how the response team will follow up.

  8. Inform the conference chair that a report exists, without personal details unless the chair is part of the decision. Escalate through the route agreed in advance with the Foundation.

What history says#

  • The Foundation expects the conference to adhere to the Plone Code of Conduct, publish it, and have ticket buyers agree to it (R-013, verified-current as of 2026-11-02, in requirements-register). The Committee's schedule asks for the code on the website from the start and for agreement at ticket purchase (L-4027, L-4037).

  • In 2013 acknowledging the code of conduct was mandatory for attendees, speakers and exhibitors for the first time, as a condition of a sponsor (L-3102 in code-of-conduct-operations#case-notes).

  • In 2016 the pre-arrival briefing put the Code of Conduct response contact and the emergency number at the top (L-4135 in code-of-conduct-operations#case-notes).

  • The archive holds no published account of how a report at a Plone conference was handled, which is as it should be; the steps above follow general incident-response practice, not a recorded case.
