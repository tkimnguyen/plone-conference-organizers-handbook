Legal entity and financial agent#

Why it matters#

Someone has to sign the venue contract, open the bank account, sell the tickets and carry a loss. In most past Plone conferences that someone was the organizers themselves, using their own bank accounts, credit and accounting. They carried the full risk of missed attendance or cost overruns and kept any surplus (L-4056).

The Foundation offers another route. As financial agent it covers losses and lends its banking, accounting and Treasurer, and in return it takes part in the planning and keeps the final say and the surplus. The route changes who carries the risk, who holds the money and which tax regime applies.

These are the two organisational models: the Foundation carries the risk and the Board sets the budget and planning rules, or the organiser carries the risk and negotiates its freedoms. The Foundation requirements register is the checklist for that negotiation, and the agreed points belong in a short letter of intent per edition.

The choice is easy to postpone and hard to reverse. A team that signs contracts first and chooses the entity later may find that the entity it picked cannot do what the contracts require. It may also find that the people who fronted the money carry a loss the team never agreed to share.

Applies when#

  • jurisdiction The legal entity, its tax regime and the rules for nonprofit status depend on the country where it is based.

  • budget The financial-agent route, a seed loan and an own-risk budget lead to different obligations and different exposure.

  • organizer A company, a volunteer group and a Foundation-led team differ in whether they have an entity of their own.

Decisions you must make#

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-3116, L-4138) Which entity signs contracts, holds the bank account and sells tickets? Name it before signing anything. Two conferences used a partner organization for the legal and financial role, a national nonprofit association in one case and a cooperative supplier in the other. The entity also decides the tax regime, so check it with a local adviser before pricing tickets. (decide-by: T-12m)

  • MUST (req: R-007; as-of: 2026-11-02) Does your bid state explicitly whether you ask the Foundation to act as financial agent? The Foundation's financial policy (S091) requires the statement, and the 2018 call was the first to carry the conditions as formal requirements. State yes or no in the bid. (decide-by: T-12m)

  • MUST (req: R-008; as-of: 2026-11-02) If the Foundation acts as financial agent, have you accepted its terms on risk, accounts and extra hours? This item binds only when you choose the agent route. The Foundation assumes the financial risk and lends its bank and PayPal accounts, its accounting and its Treasurer, and it does not pay the organizers' extra work hours (L-4058). Whether the offer is still extended in practice is unconfirmed as of 2026-11-02. (decide-by: T-12m)

  • MUST (req: R-009; as-of: 2026-11-02) If the Foundation acts as financial agent, have you agreed what you owe in exchange? This item binds only when you choose the agent route. The organizers send the Board regular updates, at least one Board member joins the planning, the Board has final authority on all financial decisions and the Foundation receives any surplus. Put the agreement in writing. (decide-by: T-12m)

  • MUST (req: R-010; as-of: 2026-11-02) Have you budgeted the Foundation's share of registration revenue and confirmed the rate in writing? The Foundation's calls named 15% of per-person registration revenue (S059), with 20% in the 2018 call (S055), and the 2023 board minutes record a 10% donation by one organizer (S070). As of 2026-11-02 no standing document states the current rate or its form. (decide-by: T-9m)

  • OPTION Do you need an advance or seed loan, and have you asked the Board? As of 2026-11-02 no live Foundation document offers a loan, and the agent policy mentions none. Ask the Board in writing before you plan on one. (decide-by: T-12m)

  • OPTION Can a nonprofit or university rent the venue on your behalf? The 2011 team wrote that renting under nonprofit status could be the difference between a loss and breaking even or better (L-3061). The effect on ticket tax is a jurisdiction question for a local adviser. (decide-by: T-9m)

How-to#

Choose the entity#

  1. List the candidates: your own company or association, a partner nonprofit or university, a cooperative acting as a supplier, and the Foundation as financial agent. Done when each candidate has a one-line answer to four questions: who signs, who holds the account, who sells tickets, who carries a loss.

  2. Ask the Foundation Board in writing whether it will act as financial agent for your event and whether it will advance funds. Done when you hold a dated written reply.

  3. State your choice in the bid, yes or no on the agent route.

  4. If you choose the agent route, write down the terms on regular updates, the Board member in the planning, final authority, the surplus and who pays for extra work hours. Done when both sides have the text.

  5. Check the tax regime with a local adviser before you set ticket prices. The selling entity decides the regime, and the finance chapter covers the consequences.

Pay for things#

  1. Decide how every payment is made before the first invoice: through the agent's accounts, through a conference account or by reimbursement.

  2. If you use reimbursement, agree the batch size, the receipt rules and the turnaround in writing before anyone spends their own money (L-3005).

  3. Ask suppliers to invoice the entity directly, not a person. Done when no supplier invoice carries a private name.

  4. Keep scans of every receipt with a list of what was bought and why from the first purchase.

  5. Agree in writing who covers the organizers' extra work hours, because an agent does not.

After the conference#

  1. Settle surplus or loss as agreed and deliver the financial summary to the next team. See Handover contract.

Risks#

Organizers front costs personally and are reimbursed late or not at all

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: high | mitigation: Pay through the agent's or the conference's account where possible, and agree batch size, receipt rules and turnaround in writing before anyone spends private money | owner-role: treasurer | playbook: cash-flow-crunch

A lead organizer who pays everything personally carries the cash-flow gap and any loss.

The organizers' extra work hours are not covered

risk | likelihood: high | impact: medium | mitigation: Agree in writing before accepting the agent route who pays the organizers' hours, and budget a fee or accept the cost explicitly | owner-role: lead organizer

The Foundation's policy states that the added work hours will not be covered (L-4059).

The Foundation's agent offer is unavailable when you ask

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: high | mitigation: Ask the Board in writing before the bid and keep a second entity option ready | owner-role: lead organizer

Practice is unconfirmed as of 2026-11-02. The policy text is live, but no document says whether the offer is extended in practice.

The team has no entity that can sign and hold money

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: high | mitigation: Secure a partner nonprofit, a supplier cooperative or the agent route before any contract is signed | owner-role: lead organizer

A volunteer group without a legal entity cannot hold an account or sign a contract.

The chosen entity produces an unexpected tax regime

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: high | mitigation: Check the tax regime with a local adviser before pricing tickets, and compare entities by the final price a participant pays | owner-role: treasurer

The selling entity decides the regime, and the regime changes the price participants pay.
