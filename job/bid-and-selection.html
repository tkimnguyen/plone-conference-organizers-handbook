Bid and selection#

Why it matters#

A Plone conference is awarded, not simply started. Only the Plone Foundation can approve an event as "Plone Conference " or "International Plone Conference", and it selects the location (L-4054, S006, as of 2026-11-02). A team that skips this step can end up with a fully planned event that may not carry the name its audience searches for.

The bid is also the first place a team writes down its budget, its people and its promises. The proposals of earlier editions show how much a good bid has to cover, from venue and fees to marketing and visas. Starting from them saves weeks.

The process itself is a weak spot. The hosting calls from 2016 to 2020 describe it in detail, and no hosting call after 2020 has been located as of 2026-11-02. The current selection process is therefore undocumented, and this chapter says so rather than guess.

Applies when#

  • all Every conference that wants the Foundation-approved name goes through approval, whatever its size.

Decisions you must make#

  • OPTION Will you hold a regional event before bidding to show you can deliver? A 2015 symposium of about 70 people preceded one bid (L-4118). (decide-by: T-18m)

  • MUST (req: R-001; as-of: 2026-11-02) Will the event carry the Foundation-approved name, and has the Board approved it? Without approval the event may not be called "Plone Conference " or "International Plone Conference". (decide-by: T-14m)

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-4074, L-4083) Will you contact the Board before writing the proposal and follow the Board's selection process? The calls of 2016 and 2019 invite early contact, then the Board pre-qualifies complete proposals and Foundation members vote. That description is unconfirmed for later years, so confirm it in writing. (decide-by: T-14m)

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-4075, L-4084) When will your proposal be due, and does your timeline fit the call? The calls of 2015 and 2018 announced the location about twelve and eleven months before the conference. (decide-by: T-14m)

  • OPTION Will you merge the conference with a national or regional partner event? In 2013 the organizers merged with a national Python conference to cut costs (L-3092). Partner events change the audience, see defining-success. (decide-by: T-14m)

  • MUST (req: R-007; as-of: 2026-11-02) Does the proposal state whether you ask the Foundation to be the financial agent? The 2019 call frames the offer as a trade of loss risk for oversight. See legal-entity-and-financial-agent. (decide-by: T-13m)

  • DEFAULT (basis: L-2054, L-2072, L-3111) Which earlier proposals will you use as your outline? Teams in 2010, 2012 and 2013 built bids around earlier published proposals and similar section lists. (decide-by: T-13m)

How-to#

Before you write#

  1. Ask the Board whether it will approve your event under the Foundation name. Done looks like a written answer you store in the Tailoring Record.

  2. Contact the Board early so it can advise; the 2016 call invites exactly this (L-4074, S049). Done looks like a first conversation and a named point of contact.

  3. Ask in the same message how the current selection works and whether another call for proposals will be issued. Done looks like a written description of the process, or a recorded "unknown" that you carry as a risk.

  4. Agree your advisers and your organizer type with the steps in stakeholder-map. Done looks like a short list of advisers who have read your outline.

Write and submit#

  1. Read two or three earlier proposals and copy the section list of the closest one, as the 2012 and 2013 teams did. Done looks like an outline with one heading per section and an owner for each.

  2. Cover the elements the 2016 to 2020 calls required, even though none is confirmed as current: a complete budget (R-004), a cash-flow plan (R-006), the key people with their track record (R-026) and a marketing and community plan (R-027), all as of 2026-11-02 and all unverifiable. Done looks like one section per element.

  3. State whether you ask the Foundation to act as financial agent, and what you accept in return, as in the 2019 call (L-4083, S059). Done looks like one paragraph the treasurer and the Board have both read.

  4. Add local support where it helps, for example a letter from a public body or a host institution. Done looks like an attached letter or a recorded decision to skip it.

  5. Submit the proposal as the Board asks; the calls of 2016 to 2020 asked for a PDF or ODF file (R-003, as of 2026-11-02). Done looks like a dated confirmation of receipt.

After the award#

  1. Agree with the Board when and how the next host is announced, and fix the dates with the venue before the announcement. Done looks like a date for the reveal and signed-off conference dates.

  2. Record the award conditions in the Tailoring Record. Done looks like a list of what the Board expects from you and what you expect from it.

Principles and general lessons#

[general]

lesson | scope: general | source: S019, S036, S094 | ledger: L-2054, L-2072, L-3111 | confidence: observed | status: active | basis: independent-conferences

Earlier proposals are the best template for a bid. They cover the same ground: organizers and track record, city and venue, schedule and fees, travel, marketing and local support. Study two or three before you outline your own.

[general]

lesson | scope: general | source: S046, S094, S095 | ledger: L-3111, L-4118, L-4156 | confidence: reported | status: active | basis: independent-conferences

Show that your team can deliver. Bids named the team's track record with national and regional conferences, and one team ran a regional event first.

The call timelines of 2015 and 2018 come from one organizing body and stay in Case notes without promotion.

Risks#

The event is announced under the Foundation name before approval

risk | likelihood: low | impact: high | mitigation: Obtain the Board's written approval of the name and the location before any public announcement | owner-role: lead organizer

An approved name and location are the Board's to give, and an announcement made before approval can be withdrawn.

The selection process has changed since the last published call

risk | likelihood: medium | impact: medium | mitigation: Ask the Board for the current process and the deadlines in writing before you start the bid | owner-role: lead organizer

The last hosting call located dates from 2020, so a team that follows the 2016 to 2020 pattern may meet a different process.

Announced dates move after the public reveal

risk | likelihood: low | impact: medium | mitigation: Fix the dates with the venue and the key partners before the reveal | owner-role: lead organizer | playbook: venue-loss

At one edition the dates announced with the next host city were later moved, which undoes plans made by early planners among attendees.

Thin content#

Current selection process is undocumented

thin | kind: gap | needs: A written description of how hosting proposals are submitted, pre-qualified and awarded today, and whether another call will be issued | who-might-know: Foundation Board; Conference Committee | area: other | since: 2026-11-02

The requirements register marks the selection process as unverifiable, because the hosting calls of 2016 to 2020 are the latest located.

CFP-era hard requirements may no longer apply

thin | kind: gap | needs: Confirmation from the Board whether the date window, venue size, airport distance and catering requirements of the 2016 to 2020 calls still bind a bid | who-might-know: Foundation Board; Conference Committee | area: other | since: 2026-11-02

The calls lowered the minimum venue capacity once in the archived record (L-4080, S055), and the 2026 debrief puts the recurring community near one hundred people (S098).

Bids and selections after 2020 are not archived

thin | kind: gap | needs: Proposals and selection accounts of the editions after 2020, where they exist and can be shared | who-might-know: Past organizers; Foundation Board | area: other | since: 2026-11-02

The evidence in this chapter ends with the 2023 reveal.
