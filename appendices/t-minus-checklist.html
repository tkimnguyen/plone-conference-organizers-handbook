T-minus checklist#

This page is the master countdown. It runs from eighteen months before the event to 100 days after it, one row per window. Each row says what must be true by then, who usually owns it, and where in the handbook the advice lives. Use it as the skeleton of your tracker: copy the rows in, turn every window into a date, and give every row an owner and a deputy.

The page does not repeat the chapters. Every row points at a chapter section, written as the chapter id, a hash sign and the section slug, such as master-timeline#how-to. Read the section before you tick the row.

How the windows are counted#

  • T-minus counts back from the first event day, including trainings. T-12m means twelve months before it, T-8w eight weeks and T-17d seventeen days.

  • T-plus counts forward from the last event day, as in The annual ritual.

  • The month windows follow the Plone Foundation's month-by-month planning schedule, which assumes a September conference. January is then about T-8m, February T-7m, March T-6m, and so on. Shift every row if your event falls in another month (S004).

  • A window is a deadline, not a start date. Work that needs a long lead starts earlier.

The spine is six months at minimum. The 2006 Seattle team recommended starting the planning process six months ahead and called an early start essential (L-1009). That conference ran from first idea to event in about six months, and the later conferences in the record ran longer when they could (L-1011). This table assumes a year of runway. If yours is shorter, keep the order of the rows and compress the gaps.

Rhythm#

Set the meeting cadence in the first meeting. The Foundation's planning schedule starts with an initial meeting in November or December of the preceding year. It then meets monthly, every two weeks from late spring, and weekly in the last month (L-4026).

What to have on hand#

Two conferences wrote down the physical kit that saved them. Put a packing list for the load-in on the T-2d row from these two lists.

  • The 2011 reusable kit: network cables, floor tape for cables and a small switch, easels and wall pads, power cords, sticky notes for open-space sign-up, pens, HD webcams, lanyards and badge holders, card stock, glue and a hole punch for last-minute signs, stickers and stamps (L-3042).

  • The 2011 have-on-hand list: sturdy archive boxes, a printer and paper, spare batteries of all kinds, pens, several markers, spare presentation adapters, one or two network switches, good floor tape for cables, and snacks for volunteers and anyone with low blood sugar (L-3070).

The six-month spine#

If you start late, the Seattle spine shows the minimum order of work in months. It is a record of one conference, not a model to copy (L-1011).

  1. Reserve the venue in mid-May. venue#how-to

  2. Run a community survey in late May and win the bid and the Foundation award in early June. bid-and-selection#how-to, defining-success#decisions-you-must-make

  3. Form the program committee and sign the first sponsors in June. cfp-and-selection#how-to, sponsors#how-to

  4. Open registration in mid-July after a three-week call for proposals. ticketing-and-registration#how-to, cfp-and-selection#how-to

  5. Select sessions in mid-August. scheduling#how-to

  6. Arrange catering and video in early September. catering#how-to, av-recording-internet#how-to

  7. Print in early October. print-and-merch#how-to

How to use the checklist#

  1. Copy the table into your tracker. Done when each row is an issue with a due date, an owner and a deputy.

  2. Convert every window into a calendar date from the first event day. Done when no row has a window instead of a date.

  3. Read each linked section before you tick the row. The row is a reminder, not the advice.

  4. Mark a row skipped, with a reason, in your Tailoring Record instead of deleting it.

  5. Add rows for your own contracts and supplier lead times. The handbook rows are the shared floor.

  6. Review the whole table at every freeze date, and again at the end of the event to feed the post-mortem.

Applies when#

Use the checklist for every conference. Drop the rows for components you do not run, such as trainings, a side track or a stream, and say so in the Tailoring Record.

Thin content#

Lead times are anchored on one season

thin | kind: gap | needs: Lead times for conferences outside September and for events with a longer or shorter runway, and the owners each team actually used | who-might-know: Organizers of past conferences held in other months
