Glossary#

This glossary lists the terms that the handbook uses in a specific sense, and conference words that a first-time organizer may not know. Each entry is short and points to the page that holds the full treatment. Pages that are not yet written are named in backticks by their identifier. Terms are in alphabetical order.

A to C#

Age badge The label on a specific lesson that shows its year, place and age in whole years, for example [specific · 2012 · Examplecity · 14 y]. It is computed from the pinned as-of year, never typed by hand. See How to read lessons.

Annex A page for content that cannot be one-size-fits-all, such as notes on tax and legal points for one country. Every annex opens with a disclaimer: it is general information, not legal, tax or insurance advice. See Tailor.

As-of date The date a fact, a figure or a requirement was last verified. The handbook states prices, rates and counts only with an as-of date and a source. A MUST badge carries the as-of date of the register row it cites.

BoF A birds-of-a-feather session: an informal, self-organized discussion on a shared topic. Seattle 2006 steered rejected proposers into lightning talks and BoFs (L-1069). See programme-portfolio and Scheduling.

Business track A side track aimed at decision-makers and organizations rather than at developers, held in parallel with the developer conference. The 2026 edition ran as a one-day track in the conference week. See Business and side tracks.

Calendar versioning (CalVer) The way the handbook names its releases, by year and a counter, for example 2026.1. The tag marks the state of the handbook that a team used, so that its Tailoring Record can cite it.

Case note A specific lesson, placed in the section Case notes of a chapter, newest first. It says what one conference did at one time and place.

CFP Call for proposals. Two different calls carry the abbreviation: the call that invites talks and trainings from speakers, and the Foundation's call that invites bids to host a conference. The handbook says which one it means. See CFP and selection and Bid and selection.

Confidence How the evidence behind a lesson reached its source: observed, reported or hearsay. A lesson shows the weakest confidence among the ledger rows it cites. Hearsay never supports a MUST or a DEFAULT.

Conference Committee The Plone community group, made up of past organizers, that gives organizing teams continuity between editions and a place to ask questions. It is reached through a public role address on the Foundation's site. It is not the same body as the organizing team of a given conference. See Team and governance.

Context dimension One of seven properties used to describe a conference: size, jurisdiction, organizer type, budget model, venue type, format and language. Chapters and lessons use them to say which conferences they fit. See Context dimensions.

Coverage map A generated page that shows, for each chapter, its status, maturity, thin markers, lessons and risks, and the count of thin markers per predicted gap area. It is how the handbook shows its own weak spots.

D to L#

Decision register A generated page that lists every decision in the handbook with its level badge, in chapter order. Teams use it as the checklist for their Tailoring Record.

DEFAULT The middle normative level. A strong recommendation backed by at least two ledger rows from at least two distinct conferences. You may deviate if you record the reason in your Tailoring Record. See How to read lessons.

Deputy The second person or team accountable for a page, named in its front matter, so that a page is never one person away from orphaned. See Governance.

Drift check A continuous integration step that regenerates the generated pages and fails the build if they differ from the committed copies.

Financial agent The model in which the Plone Foundation acts as the financial agent for a conference: it covers losses and lends its accounts and treasurer, and in return takes part in the planning and keeps the final say and the surplus. The archive records trial uses from 2011 and a standing offer from 2018 (L-4057). See Legal entity and financial agent.

General lesson A timeless claim that passed the promotion rule. It appears in Principles and general lessons, carries no date, place or organizer, and ranks above specific lessons.

Generator The script that computes lesson titles and classes and writes the generated pages. See Update.

Handover contract The list of what an outgoing organizing team owes the next one, and by when, so that knowledge and assets do not leave with the team. It is a proposal until the community accepts it. See Handover contract.

Ledger The table of evidence rows behind the handbook. Each row is one extraction from one source, with an ID of the form L-1234, a scope, a confidence and a disposition that says where it landed. See Lessons ledger.

Lesson One transferable statement with its provenance, written as a block in a chapter. Lessons are general or specific. See Lesson authoring rules.

Lightning talk A very short talk, often a few minutes, on an open or last-minute slot. Arnhem 2012 advised three to four minutes, not seven (L-2099). See Scheduling.

M to R#

Maturity How far a page has come: stub, draft, reviewed or proven. A page never claims more than its evidence supports.

MUST The strongest normative level. A Foundation requirement or a legal necessity, citing a row of the requirements register and its as-of date. Deviation needs a written Foundation exception, and a legal necessity cannot be waived by the handbook.

OPTION The weakest normative level. The choice depends on context, and nothing needs recording except the choice.

Open space A session format with no fixed agenda, in which participants propose and run their own topics. Seattle 2006 ran its sprint on open-space principles (L-1154). A strategic open space at Bristol 2014 produced a community roadmap (L-4111).

Overlay The tailoring method of the handbook. A team keeps the text as written and records its deviations in a Tailoring Record, instead of forking the handbook. See Tailor.

Plone Conference The name of the annual international conference of the Plone community. Only the Foundation approves its use, and there is only one Plone Conference. Regional events are named after a country or a US state, and they used to be called symposia, a name dropped in 2012 to avoid misunderstanding (L-4055). Historical sources therefore say "symposium" for what the 2012 amendment calls a regional event. See the requirements register, rows R-001 and R-002.

Plone Foundation The non-profit that owns the Plone trademarks. It selects the conference location, sets the rules for using the name and, when asked, can act as financial agent. Foundation-led editions exist, but most editions are organized by a company or a group of volunteers. The handbook is advisory and is not Foundation policy.

Playbook A short incident page in Part 3 with triggers, first-hour actions, owner roles and links to the chapters that hold the full advice. Playbooks link to chapters and do not restate them.

Promotion The step by which a specific lesson becomes a general one. It needs three independent conferences across two organizing bodies, or a structural reason that does not depend on the year. See Lesson authoring rules.

Requirements register The table of Foundation requirements for conferences, with source, status and as-of date per row. It backs the MUST badge. See Foundation requirements register.

Risk One thing that can go wrong, written as an event with likelihood, impact and a mitigation. Each chapter carries its own risks; the generated risk register collects them. See Risk register.

S to Z#

Seed loan A loan from the Foundation to an organizer for up-front costs, repaid after the conference. Seattle 2006 is the archived example (L-1031). Whether the Foundation offers one as of 2026-11 is a question in the requirements register.

Source ID An identifier of the form S042 for one archived source. Chapters list every source ID they use under Sources and further reading. See Source bibliography.

Specific lesson A claim from one conference at one time and place. It appears only in Case notes and carries a year, a place and an organizing body.

Sprint The days after the conference in which participants work together on code and documentation, usually in open space. It is how newcomers become contributors, and the Foundation has its own funding route for it. See Sprints.

Stale-prone The word added to the title of a specific lesson older than eight years. It does not mean the lesson is wrong, only that its context has probably moved.

Status The state of a lesson: active, contested or superseded. A contested lesson links a discussion issue, and a superseded lesson names its replacement.

Steward The person or team accountable for a page's accuracy, named in its front matter. Stewardship is a proposal until the community accepts it. See Governance.

T-minus Time counted back from the first conference day, as in "decide by T-6m". The d, w and m units stand for days, weeks and months.

Tailoring Record The table in which a team records, chapter by chapter, whether it adopted, adapted, skipped or replaced the advice, with a reason. It is filed at handover and becomes evidence for the next update. See Tailor.

Thin marker A public note inside a chapter that says where the evidence is weak, what would close the gap and which roles might know. Its kind is gap, single-source or stale. See About the thin registry.

Thin registry The generated page that collects every thin marker in the handbook. It tells readers how far to trust a page and tells contributors where help matters most. See Thin registry.

Training A paid, hands-on course held before the conference days, with a trainer and prerequisites announced in advance. Seattle 2006 called its trainings one of the biggest reasons for the conference's success (L-1089). See Trainings.
